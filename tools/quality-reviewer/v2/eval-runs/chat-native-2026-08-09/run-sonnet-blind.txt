WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The recommendation asks Client D to do exactly what its own financial position rules out. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 3, slide 6, slide 8
   Quote (slide 3): "Client D has no cash reserves and cannot take on new fixed costs without
   new income first."
   Quote (slide 3): "The board has been clear that hiring is not possible until revenue grows."
   Quote (slide 6): "Open a retail shopfront within six months and hire three paid repair staff
   to meet demand."
   Quote (slide 8): "The plan is low-risk."
   Why it costs the client: Slide 3 says new fixed costs and hiring wait for revenue growth.
   Slide 6 recommends both, up front, before revenue has moved. The deck never says where the
   lease and salary money comes from, and slide 8 calls this "low-risk" without addressing the
   conflict, so the client cannot tell which instruction the board should follow.
   What to do: Before proposing the shop and the hires, show the financing that bridges "no
   cash reserves" to a signed lease and three salaries. If none exists, sequence the plan so
   revenue moves first, in line with the board's own condition, or name the bridge funding that
   resolves the conflict.
   Answer first: Where does the cash for the lease and the first months of three salaries come
   from, given slide 3 states there are no reserves and the board has ruled out hiring and new
   fixed costs until revenue grows.

2. The revenue projection the whole plan rests on has no working shown. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 5, slide 7
   Quote (slide 5): "Comparable social enterprises have doubled revenue after opening a shop."
   Quote (slide 7): "Revenue will double in year one. The shop pays for itself by month nine."
   Why it costs the client: The client is asked to commit to a lease and three hires on a
   doubling assumption sourced to unnamed "comparable" organisations, with no numbers, sector or
   scale given, and the month nine payback claim has no arithmetic behind it at all.
   What to do: Name the peer enterprises or the source behind "comparable," or show the
   arithmetic (current revenue, projected shop revenue, cost base) behind the doubling and the
   payback date. Until then, treat both figures as unverified projections rather than evidence.
   Answer first: What are the actual figures, and whose, behind "comparable social enterprises
   have doubled revenue," and what calculation produces "pays for itself by month nine."

3. No single sentence states the recommendation and why it solves self-sustainability. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 6, slide 7 (the reasoning is assembled across both)
   Quote (slide 6): "Open a retail shopfront within six months and hire three paid repair staff
   to meet demand."
   Quote (slide 6): "This will make Client D self-sustaining."
   Quote (slide 7): "Revenue will double in year one."
   Why it costs the client: Reading only the titles and opening lines, slide by slide, a reader
   gets a sequence of separate facts: situation, constraint, market, opportunity, action, impact,
   confidence, steps. No one sentence states what to do and why it closes the two-year
   self-sustainability gap; the reader has to build that link themselves from three slides.
   What to do: Write one slide, early, that states the recommendation and the reason it closes
   the gap in a single sentence, then let the rest of the deck support that sentence instead of
   building toward it.
   Answer first: If a board member reads only the slide titles and first sentences, what one
   sentence should they land on, and where does that sentence currently sit.

4. Next steps gives three verbs and nothing to act on. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 9
   Quote: "Sign a lease. Recruit staff. Launch."
   Why it costs the client: A near-final deck should leave Client D able to start executing.
   Three verbs carry no owner, no budget, no measure and no order tied to the cash constraint
   named on slide 3, so the client is left to build the actual plan themselves.
   What to do: Attach an owner, a cost and a timeframe to each step, and sequence them against
   the financing question raised in finding 1 rather than listing the three eventual actions.
   Answer first: Who signs the lease, what does it cost, and what has to be true first, given
   Client D has no cash reserves today.

THE HABIT TO BUILD
The weak habit: writing the recommendation slide from the opportunity, without checking it
against the constraints slide already in the same deck. Exercise: after drafting a
recommendation, reread every constraint stated earlier in the document and write one sentence
naming the resource the recommendation needs and where it comes from, before writing the next
steps slide.
