# R-001 - AI platform partnership pathways

## For future Claude
This note evaluates how 180DC could obtain organizational access, training, or a formal relationship with Anthropic, OpenAI, or Cursor. Research is current as of 2026-06-19 and relies on official provider and 180DC pages; verify prices, eligibility, and program availability before outreach.

The central distinction is between discounted access, a campus program, and a formal services partnership. These require very different levels of organizational proof.

## Executive finding

The most realistic near-term route is not to ask immediately for a formal provider partnership. Run a controlled branch pilot through an available nonprofit, education, student, or team plan; create evidence of responsible adoption and useful outcomes; then seek global 180DC sponsorship for an in-kind technology partnership.

180DC already has a relevant precedent: its global partners page states that monday.com provides its enterprise product to the Global Leadership Team and branch network free of charge. This demonstrates that a technology-access partnership fits the organization's existing partnership model.

Source: https://www.180dc.org/our-partners (accessed 2026-06-19).

## What “partnership” can mean

### 1. Organizational access

The provider offers discounted or centrally administered seats. This is a procurement and adoption relationship, not necessarily a public strategic partnership.

### 2. Campus or community program

The provider supports student-led workshops, clubs, events, speakers, credits, or ambassadors. This is likely the easiest route to direct provider engagement.

### 3. Technology sponsorship

The provider supplies seats, credits, training, speakers, or support in exchange for structured adoption, feedback, social-impact reach, talent visibility, or a case study.

### 4. Formal services or implementation partnership

The organization is recognized as capable of advising customers on the provider and delivering implementations. This requires training, certifications, customer-delivery evidence, governance, and repeatable capability. It is the least realistic starting point for one branch.

## Anthropic routes

### Claude for Nonprofits

Anthropic currently offers nonprofit Team pricing for eligible registered nonprofits and international equivalents. Its official page lists a Team route for organizations purchasing fewer than 20 seats at $8 per user per month, with a two-seat minimum, centralized administration, Claude Code, Claude Cowork, and nonprofit resources. Eligibility is verified through Goodstack. Larger organizations contact sales.

Whether a local 180DC branch qualifies independently depends on its legal entity and relationship to the global NGO. Do not assume eligibility without verification.

Sources:

- https://claude.com/solutions/nonprofits
- https://validate.poweredbypercent.com/

### Claude for Education

Claude for Education is positioned as a university-wide plan for students, faculty, and staff, with training resources and implementation support. This route would normally require university-level sponsorship rather than a student society purchasing it alone.

Source: https://claude.com/solutions/education

### Claude Campus programs

Anthropic’s Claude Builder Clubs explicitly support student-driven AI initiatives. The official program describes collaboration with Anthropic, technical and non-technical workshops with guest speakers, hackathons, resources such as API credits, and a paid program stipend. The Spring 2026 application round is closed as of 2026-06-19, but future updates are expected.

This is a strong route for Manuel personally or for an AI-builder community, but it is not the same as giving every 180DC consultant an organizational Claude workspace.

Source: https://claude.com/programs/campus

### Claude Partner Network

Anthropic’s Partner Network targets organizations building enterprise AI practices for customers. It offers training, technical support, co-investment, certification, and a services-partner directory for firms that deliver Claude strategy and implementation.

This could become relevant to 180DC globally after multiple documented implementations, but it is premature for a branch that has not yet completed a controlled internal pilot.

Sources:

- https://claude.com/partners
- https://claude.com/partners/services

## OpenAI and Codex routes

### OpenAI for Nonprofits

OpenAI currently offers eligible nonprofits ChatGPT Business for $8 per user per month when purchased annually or $10 monthly. Larger nonprofit deployments may receive up to a 75% ChatGPT Enterprise discount through sales. Goodstack verifies eligibility.

ChatGPT Business includes a dedicated workspace, shared projects, custom workspace GPTs, administration, and Codex access. OpenAI states that Business and Enterprise data are not used to train its models.

The nonprofit discount currently applies to ChatGPT Business and Enterprise, not API usage.

Sources:

- https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9359041-do-you-offer-nonprofit-pricing
- https://openai.com/business/chatgpt-pricing/

### ChatGPT Edu

ChatGPT Edu is a university deployment route covering students, faculty, researchers, and campus operations. It includes data analysis, browsing, file integrations, document summarization, and shareable custom GPTs. Like Claude for Education, it generally requires university-level engagement.

Source: https://chatgpt.com/business/education/

### Codex

Codex is included within current ChatGPT Business capabilities. It is primarily useful for the small AI-building team, technical projects, automation, and maintaining code or structured local workflows. It is not by itself the general-purpose adoption layer for every consultant.

Source: https://openai.com/business/

## Cursor routes

### Student access

Cursor currently offers eligible university students one free year of Cursor Pro. This is the simplest way for individual technical team members to experiment.

Source: https://cursor.com/students

### Teams and Enterprise

Cursor Teams is currently listed at $40 per user per month and includes centralized billing, internal rules and skills, cloud agents, analytics, privacy mode, and SSO. Enterprise adds advanced controls, audit logs, account management, and security features.

Cursor is an engineering tool. A partnership is compelling only if 180DC has a real software-building curriculum, technical builder cohort, or maintained codebase. It is a poor default tool for non-technical consultants.

Sources:

- https://cursor.com/pricing
- https://cursor.com/enterprise
- https://cursor.com/contact-sales

## Comparable adoption cases

These examples do not prove that the same results would occur in 180DC. They show common adoption patterns.

### monday.com and 180DC

180DC states that monday.com provides enterprise access free to its Global Leadership Team and branch network. The partnership supports scalable operations through automations and integrations.

Pattern: global organizational sponsorship, in-kind product access, operational use, and mission alignment.

Source: https://www.180dc.org/our-partners

### Anthropic small-business examples

Anthropic highlights Purity Coffee, MidCentral Energy, and Simple Modern as small-business users. Their reported value centers on identifying overlooked problems, reducing clerical work, and broad daily adoption. Anthropic’s small-business package combines a desktop product, plugins, common automations, connectors, tutorials, and an AI-fluency course.

Pattern: connect existing tools, begin with repetitive work, train users, preserve human decision ownership, and expand after daily value is visible.

Source: https://claude.com/solutions/small-business

### Anthropic nonprofit examples

Anthropic reports that IDinsight uses Claude to prepare surveys faster, prototype dashboards, and draft documentation; MyFriendBen uses Claude-powered agents across public-benefit programs. These are bounded workflows with measurable outputs rather than generic “use AI more” initiatives.

Pattern: select repeatable workflows, pair adoption with evidence, and measure concrete operational outcomes.

Source: https://claude.com/solutions/nonprofits

### Cursor organizational rollouts

Cursor’s published customer examples show adoption spreading from initial users to larger engineering groups, with centralized controls and analytics added at scale. These cases are mostly larger engineering organizations, so they are weak analogues for a student consultancy but useful for the rollout sequence.

Pattern: enthusiastic technical users first, proof of workflow value, then team standardization, security controls, and centralized administration.

Sources:

- https://cursor.com/customers
- https://cursor.com/enterprise

## What providers need to see

A credible proposal should demonstrate:

- A legitimate organization and accountable internal sponsor
- A clearly defined user group
- Specific workflows, not generic enthusiasm
- A training and acceptable-use plan
- Client-confidentiality and data-governance rules
- Success measures and feedback collection
- Named administrators and support owners
- Evidence that members will actually use the product
- A reason the relationship advances the provider’s education, social-impact, talent, community, or product-feedback goals
- Appropriate approval from the branch, global 180DC, and university where applicable

| Provider route | Fit now | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropic nonprofit Team | Strong if legal eligibility is verified | General consultant workspace, learning, research, documents, and Claude Code/Cowork |
| OpenAI nonprofit Business | Strong if legal eligibility is verified | General workspace, custom GPTs, research, data analysis, and Codex for builders |
| Anthropic Campus program | Strong future application | Workshops, speakers, builder community, credits, and direct feedback |
| University Claude/ChatGPT education plan | Medium, institution-dependent | University-wide access and governance |
| Cursor student Pro | Strong for eligible technical individuals | Building and maintaining software |
| Cursor Teams partnership | Narrow fit | A defined technical builder cohort |
| Anthropic formal Partner Network | Weak now, possible later globally | Delivering repeatable Claude implementations to clients |
