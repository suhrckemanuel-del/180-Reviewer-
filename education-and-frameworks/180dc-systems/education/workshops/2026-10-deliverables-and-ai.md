# Workshop: better deliverables, with AI (2 hours, all consultants)

Status: **agreed shape, materials not built.** Settled with Manuel 2026-10-06 after two rejected
drafts (see "What was cut" at the bottom, so nobody re-adds it).

**The rule for the whole session:** AI makes the work better; you keep the thinking.

## The three principles

1. **Answer first.** Lead with the recommendation, then the support (Pyramid Principle / Minto).
2. **The title is the "so what".** Every slide title is a full-sentence takeaway.
   "Sign-ups by channel" is a label; "Referrals bring 3x more members than ads" is a so-what.
3. **Every claim has evidence.** Each number or claim on the slide points to where it came from.

Why these three: they match the reviewer's most frequent finding categories on the real decks
(evidence quality, analysis and insight, storyline and pyramid logic). Counts are the tool's own
findings, not human gold, so a signal, not proof. Teaching them well means the reviewer has less to
catch later.

## Agenda

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 | Why it matters | One bad slide next to the fixed version |
| 10–40 | The three principles | 10 min each: bad example, the fix, one rule to remember |
| 40–60 | Principles as prompts | Live use cases: turning each principle into a prompt (below) |
| 60–90 | Build one slide | Small dataset + client question; each person makes one slide with one chart, AI-assisted. Includes a 5-min HyperFrames visuals demo |
| 90–110 | Swap and catch | Swap with a partner, check against the three principles using the card |
| 110–120 | Wrap | Everyone leaves with the card and the prompt examples |

## Principles as prompts

Each prompt asks the AI to check or support the work, not to do it.

- **Answer first:** "Here is my slide. What is the one-sentence answer it is arguing? If you can't
  find one, tell me."
- **So what:** "Here is my chart. Give me three possible takeaways it supports and say which one the
  data backs most strongly." The consultant still writes the final title.
- **Evidence:** "List every claim on this slide and say which ones have no source."
- **Visuals:** "Make a chart of this data that proves this title: [their title]." Title first, then
  the chart.
