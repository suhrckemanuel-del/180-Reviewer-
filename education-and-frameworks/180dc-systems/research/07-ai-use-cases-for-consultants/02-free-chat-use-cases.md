# Research B: high-leverage uses of FREE chat AI across a consulting engagement

Compiled 2026-10-06 for 180 Degrees Consulting. Principle throughout: **AI supports thinking, never replaces it.** The consultant writes the first version of anything that carries judgment (problem statement, hypotheses, so-what, recommendation), then uses AI to critique, expand, check or rehearse.

Two pieces of evidence anchor the principle:
- The BCG/Harvard "jagged frontier" experiment (758 BCG consultants): on tasks inside AI's capability, consultants were about 25% faster and produced work rated about 40% better. On a task deliberately set just outside that capability, AI users were **19 percentage points less likely to be correct**, even though their answers were more persuasive ([HBS working paper](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=64700)).
- Mollick's rule: "think first, write first, meet first". Do your own draft or ideas before you open the AI ([Against "Brain Damage", One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/against-brain-damage)).

Practitioner sources agree on the same pattern: "brainstorm with AI, decide yourself", and treat the AI like a first-year analyst who is fast but has no judgment ([Slideworks](https://slideworks.io/resources/how-consulting-decks-differ-from-ai-generated-decks-and-how-to-use-ai-well)).

**Practical takeaways for 180DC:**
1. Use **NotebookLM** for anything document-grounded (desk-research packs, interview synthesis). It has the most generous free limits and citations by design.
2. Use **Claude Free** for long single documents (200K context) and critique passes.
3. Use **ChatGPT or Gemini Deep Research** sparingly: about 5 runs a month each, so ration them per project, not per person.
4. **Data:** anonymise before you paste, and turn training off.

## Top 10 highest-leverage (ranked)

1. **Red-team the recommendation (#31).** Highest stakes. It catches the holes the client would find, at near-zero cost.
2. **"So what?" ladder (#24).** Builds the single most important consulting skill while the AI asks rather than answers.
3. **MECE audit of your own issue tree (#5).** Structure errors spread through the whole engagement. AI catches overlaps reliably once you've built the tree.
4. **NotebookLM interview synthesis with disconfirming search (#15 + #16).** Primary research is where student teams add unique value. Grounded citations make the synthesis checkable.
5. **Action-title critique plus titles-only read-through (#28 + #27).** Fixes the most common deck flaw in minutes.
6. **Competing hypotheses (#6).** Prevents early anchoring, which the jagged-frontier study suggests AI fluency makes worse.
7. **Pressure-test the problem statement (#1).** The cheapest point to prevent the most expensive failure, a mis-scoped project.
8. **Client meeting roleplay (#34).** Students rarely get rehearsal time with senior people. This builds confidence and prepares the Q&A.
9. **Market-sizing triangulation (#22).** A fast independent second path exposes the assumption that matters most.
10. **Proposal overpromise check (#36).** Protects the pro bono relationship and the team's workload before anything is signed.
