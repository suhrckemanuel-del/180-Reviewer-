## Key findings

1. **AI helps most on drafting and ideation; it hurts on "find the real answer in messy numbers" tasks.** Inside the frontier, BCG consultants did 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, at >40% higher quality. On a task built around a numbers-plus-interviews trap, AI users were 19 pp less likely to be right ([Dell'Acqua et al. 2026](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/dell-acqua-et-al-2026-navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier_5c589c8c-fbb5-458f-b285-c944746cd717.pdf)). Checking whether the numbers hold up is exactly where an unchecked AI does damage.
2. **Weaker performers gain most, but only when they cannot act on bad advice unchecked.** Novices gained +34% in support work ([Brynjolfsson et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161)) and below-average BCG consultants +43%. Yet low-performing entrepreneurs did ~10% worse with a GPT-4 advisor, because they could not judge which advice to act on ([Otis et al.](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=65159)).
3. **Fabricated citations are the main documented failure in consulting.** Deloitte had two government reports in 2025 (A$440k Australia, C$1.6M Canada) with sources that do not exist and a fabricated court quote; one ended in a partial refund ([Fortune Oct 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/10/07/deloitte-ai-australia-government-report-hallucinations-technology-290000-refund), [Fortune Nov 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/11/25/deloitte-caught-fabricated-ai-generated-research-million-dollar-report-canada-government)). Checking that quotes and sources exist is a minimum safeguard.
4. **Firms' adoption numbers are high, but almost none of them are outcome measures.** Lilli: >75% monthly use, a claimed 30% time saving. Deckster: 450k edits, 40% weekly use. EYQ: ">80%". These are usage counts and firm claims; no firm has published a controlled quality comparison of its own tool ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/ai-creates-powerpoints-at-mckinsey-replacing-junior-workers/492624), [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/3491334/bcg-execs-ai-across-the-company-increased-productivity-employee-joy.html)).
5. **The firms' most-used tools fall into two groups: knowledge retrieval (Lilli, Sage, EYQ) and slide production and critique (Deckster, McKinsey's Tone of Voice).** BCG's "review this" grading of slides is reported as Deckster's most popular feature ([BI via AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/inside-ai-boom-thats-transforming-094401885.html)). Nothing reported shows these tools check the logic or numbers behind a slide.
6. **AI works like an extra teammate in ideation, but makes everyone's ideas more alike.** At P&G, one person with AI matched a two-person team without it (+0.37 SD) ([Dell'Acqua et al. 2025](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=67197)). ChatGPT-assisted brainstorms produce less varied idea sets ([Meincke et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02173-x)), and the BCG study saw the same.
7. **People cannot accurately judge whether AI is helping them.** Experienced developers were 19% slower while believing they were 20% faster ([METR 2025](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)). Higher trust in AI predicts less critical thinking ([Lee et al., CHI 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/)). Self-reported time savings, as in most firm claims, should be discounted.
8. **Unchecked AI output pushes the work onto reviewers, and onto learners' future skills.** 40% of desk workers received "workslop" in the past month, at ~2h per case, and managers are hit hardest ([CNBC/HBR 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/23/ai-generated-workslop-is-destroying-productivity-and-teams-researchers-say.html)). Students who practised with an AI that gave answers did 17% worse once it was removed; an AI that gave hints instead did no harm ([Bastani et al., PNAS 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)). For training juniors, this argues for making them attempt the work before the AI helps, and for hints rather than answers.
9. **The junior pyramid is under pressure.** McKinsey's CEO reports 25k agents alongside 40k staff and non-client-facing roles down 25% ([Fortune Jan 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/01/14/how-to-get-hired-at-mckinsey-ai-tools-liberal-arts-creativity)). Employment for 22-25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs is down 13-19% ([Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)).
10. **Enterprise-wide, adoption is outrunning profit impact.** In McKinsey's 2026 survey, 44% are scaling AI but only 37% see any EBIT effect (flat year on year). The 6% of high performers stand out mainly for redesigning workflows (~3/4 of them), not for having more tools ([McKinsey State of AI 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)).

## Caveats
- The Business Insider article was read through its AOL syndication. Some firm figures differ between articles (Lilli: 70% of 45k vs >75% of 43k; BCG custom GPTs: 6k in Aug 2024 vs 18k in 2025), probably because they were reported at different dates.
- The 2026 Organization Science version of the jagged-frontier paper was confirmed; the row 12-13 numbers come from its widely cited 2023 working-paper abstract and should be checked against the published tables.
- The PwC 10-weeks-to-10-days and McKinsey "1.5M hours" figures are vendor or CEO statements and have not been independently checked.
