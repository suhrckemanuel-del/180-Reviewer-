# Independent overall consulting review

Reviewed 7 October 2026. Perspective: engagement manager, knowledge team, client, and junior practitioner. Inputs: the completed research findings and eight candidate statements. This is a qualitative evidence and workflow review; no consultant trial, measured productivity test, or financial backtest was performed.

## Verdict

The idea deserves a bounded teaching pilot. Its strongest contribution is making research ownership visible: framing a decision, tracing facts, challenging implications, and specifying the next client question. The findings already make these distinctions well. Retain the claim ledger, source-origin checks, human framing, rejection of synthetic demand as observed evidence, and separate grading of evidence and persuasion.

The three contexts are a plausible instructional design, not an established MBB operating standard or a demonstrated improvement over one structured assistant. Commercial usefulness requires the research brief to feed an engagement decision and an explicit client-data request. A final recommendation written by a human is insufficient proof of either learning or accountable analysis.

## What the primary sources establish

I independently reopened six original sources. The dates and environments matter; these public accounts are not an inventory of every firm's current practice.

- McKinsey's Lilli implementation account documents use since its 2023 rollout and firm-reported colleague savings up to 30% for searching and synthesizing knowledge. It reports active adoption and ongoing education needs. These are the firm's claims, with no workshop-specific comparator or causal productivity estimate supplied on the page. [McKinsey implementation account](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/how-we-help-clients/rewiring-the-way-mckinsey-works-with-lilli).
- Bain's 2 October 2024 account specifically describes Python and Excel pilots, employee-built task tools, leadership sponsorship, a signed conduct code, training, and sharing through an internal marketplace. Its examples also include partner-knowledge access and due-diligence document analysis. This is more concrete than generic AI enthusiasm, but adoption and benefit claims remain self-reported. [Bain deployment account](https://www.bain.com/insights/how-to-accelerate-progress-on-ai/).
- The published BCG/HBS experiment tests GPT-4 on specified consulting exercises. It establishes task-dependent results and shows that persuasive output can coexist with incorrect analysis. It does not measure our workshop, current public search tools, or whole engagements. [Published experimental paper, abstract and sections 3–5](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/dell-acqua-et-al-2026-navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier_5c589c8c-fbb5-458f-b285-c944746cd717.pdf).
- BCG's separate 2024 account explicitly distinguishes immediate assisted performance from intrinsic reskilling. Its short task assessment found no corresponding knowledge gain; its duration and assessment design limit generalization. This supports testing learning separately, rather than assuming that long-term learning is impossible. [BCG 2024 study account, “Reskilled, but Only When Augmented”](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2024/gen-ai-increases-productivity-and-expands-capabilities).
- Kantar describes KAiA as a partner for hypotheses and narrative starting points, with analysts contributing first-hand respondent context, including nonverbal reactions. This provides a concrete human contribution to interpretation rather than a generic human sign-off. It is a company workflow description, not independent performance validation. [Kantar workflow account, “How Kantar Qual optimises AI-human collaboration”](https://www.kantar.com/Inspiration/Agile-market-research/where-human-imagination-meets-AI-scale).
- Forrester's product page describes AI discovery of proprietary research, traceable sources, experts producing research, and optional analyst inquiries. It supports a bridge from self-service access to human interpretation, not autonomous research authorship. [Forrester AI Access, “How it works” and “The difference”](https://www.forrester.com/research/ai-access/).

Together these sources support a practical pattern of bounded assistance, reusable knowledge, task-specific workflows, and human expertise. They do not establish a universal firm method, audited value for every use case, or a three-account architecture. Public product descriptions reveal selected practices; undisclosed routines and failures remain unknown.

## Three material weaknesses and changes

1. **The research-to-decision bridge is incomplete.** Add a one-page engagement brief: decision owner and deadline, options including deferral, hypotheses, decision-changing evidence, missing client data, and the analysis each dataset enables. For Northstar, request store contribution, incremental capital and operating cost, catchment and competition, cannibalization, ramp-up, and delivery constraints. Provide clearly fictional figures for a teaching exercise. Require a sensitivity or break-even explanation and an alternative-investment comparison before a recommendation.
2. **Verification risks becoming ceremonial.** Cap the combined final ledger, not just each researcher's output. Assign each material claim a human owner. Require that owner to open the source and explain the fact-to-client inference. Give the auditor explicit scope to retain valid claims and classify unresolved ones; agreement is not a pass. Add deliberately misleading but plausible claims alongside accurate ones and score false acceptance and false rejection. A critical reviewer who rejects everything is also performing poorly.
3. **Apprenticeship and adoption need operating choices.** Add an unaided pre-exercise, oral defense, and a later transfer task using a different case. Have a manager coach one revised hypothesis and one rejected inference. Ask teams to select their own recurring bottleneck and reuse the approved template. Protected practice and coaching time are design commitments; do not describe them as guaranteed job protection.

## How the workflow fits an actual engagement

These are proposed operating changes, not claims that all firms already follow them.

**Engagement manager:** agrees the decision and workplan, sets a proportionate checking budget, and owns whether the evidence is sufficient. A fast research draft has value only if it changes the next analytical step or reduces avoidable rework.

**Knowledge team:** supplies an authorized source pack, definitions, reusable prior work, and routes to specialists. It maintains provenance and dates so teams can reuse evidence safely and detect stale benchmarks. Public chats teach these habits but cannot supply an internal corpus, licensed access, expert directory, or engagement permissions by themselves.

**Practitioner:** extracts facts, checks denominators, develops commercial mechanisms, asks client questions, and records why a hypothesis changed. Junior exposure to expert conversations and client data is part of the learning design, not an optional replacement for manual search.

**Client:** needs a decision explanation, assumptions, ranges, implementation implications, and confidence bounded by evidence. Deliver a concise evidence brief and targeted data/interview request before a polished recommendation. Make the named consultant responsible for decisive claims and commercial assumptions. Human authorship alone is not that responsibility.

For the public demonstration, use public or fictional inputs. For an engagement, translate the pattern into the firm's approved tools and actual information policies. Teach three fresh contexts or three participants without requiring three registrations. Multiple accounts add neither evidence independence nor enterprise controls.

## Pilot and unresolved evidence

Compare manual research, one structured assistant, and three contexts on matched cases; count manager review, source checking, repair, and synthesis in total human time. Score supported claims, reasonable inference, commercially useful next steps, and unaided explanation separately. The findings already propose blind review and time to an acceptable brief; retain those. Record participant experience and model/search configuration so results have an interpretable scope.

A first pilot should answer whether the added auditor catches material errors without causing excessive verification effort, whether learners can defend their conclusions, and whether the output helps a manager commission the next workstream. A later transfer check is needed before claiming retained learning. Repeat across cases before generalizing superiority.

Unresolved: relative performance of one versus three contexts; net human time; retrieval coverage; dependence on participant expertise; retention of judgment; engagement-level commercial value; actual undisclosed firm routines; and workforce effects. The eight selected statements are a diagnostic exercise, not a representative failure-rate sample. No percentage error reduction or time saving follows from this review.

## Critical thinking before, during, and after AI

Before: the junior writes a provisional hypothesis and causal mechanism unaided, states what would overturn it, and identifies what public research cannot answer. The manager checks the framing before prompting.

During: the junior opens decisive originals, tests metric comparability, reconstructs one calculation, logs a counterexample, and explains why an AI-suggested inference was accepted or rejected. An expert or client conversation addresses a gap that retrieval cannot close. The manager reviews a reasoning step, not only the final slide.

After: the junior defends the recommendation without the chat, states residual uncertainty and a decision reversal trigger, and completes a new unaided transfer task later. Compare this performance with the baseline. Allocate any verified capacity gain to coached analysis and client exposure through an explicit team workplan; preserving development depends on that management choice.
