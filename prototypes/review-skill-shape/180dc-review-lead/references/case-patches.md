# Patterns from past reviews

Each pattern came from a review that got it wrong once. Patterns only: no client, sector, figure
or quote. Read this when a candidate finding involves a scoring map, a survey or a model.

## Placements on a map or matrix

Before questioning where a case sits, identify every stated dimension, the evidence rule and the
scoring or aggregation rule, then compare cases on the same basis. Sharing one value on one
dimension does not mean two cases belong in the same place. If the method is missing, write a
question and say what cannot be concluded. Never invent a score.

## Disclosure labels

When a label like "not disclosed" matters, ask whether it describes public reporting, an internal
process or both before treating it as a gap.

## Surveys and samples

Do not treat separate surveys as independent confirmation without evidence of their design. Before
claiming a sample limit breaks a pricing or sizing decision, check which input the model actually
uses and look for the other surveys in the appendix.

## Model weights and inputs

A weight in a model does not prove that one survey statistic is the input. Trace the input's role
in the model before saying it determines the recommendation. Say what the source establishes and
what is only inferred.

## Effort estimates

Give no hours or effort estimate unless asked, and then only with a stated scope and basis.
