Each draft shows the same review of the same invented deck. Only the presentation changes. Open a draft to try it; 03, 04 and 05 let you decide each finding and build a note to the team.

02PaperGood direction

The deck as paper on a desk

Each finding is pinned to its slide: the quote sits on a 16:9 sheet with a green highlighter, and the note is clipped over the corner.

  • Main issue as a large sentence, no box.
  • Counts on a printed label; slides as a segmented strip.
  • Calm, but read-only: the lead still has to work out what to send.

Built around the lead's job

Swipe one finding per card and choose send to team, ask first, or leave out. The last card is a note to paste to the team.

  • The right idea: the lead ends with something to send.
  • Too much colour, and the big rounded cards slip back into the Claude look.

04Review sheetFront-runner

One ruled sheet, with decisions

The calm of 02 and the hand-back of 03. Findings sit on slide frames, each ends in a send / ask / leave-out row, and the sheet ends in the note.

  • Boxes are ruled cells that share one line: square corners, no shadows, no tinted panels.
  • Green only on the main issue and the lead's "send" choices.
  • Schibsted Grotesk and Martian Mono instead of Claude-like fonts.

05Deck lineNew

The deck as a transit line

The team fixes a deck in slide order, so the findings run down a line from slide 1 to the end. Each quote sits at the slide it comes from, and one problem across several slides is a numbered red loop in its own lane.

  • Tap any slide number (in the bar or on the line) to open that slide, step with ← → or a swipe, and jump to its findings.
  • Jumping to a finding fades every other loop, so you can follow one.
  • No boxes around the content; the only containers are capsule pills, the slide sheet and a ticket stub for the note.
  • The note lists the fixes in slide order. Font: Overpass, based on road-sign lettering.
Open draft 05

What we changed, and where it came from

Why the early drafts looked like Claude

  • Every block a floating card: big round corners, soft shadow, pastel panel.
  • The main issue in a tinted box with a stripe down its left edge.
  • Red, amber and grey severity chips, which read as a grade.
  • A bookish serif and plain system sans, close to Claude's own chat fonts.

What we borrowed instead

  • Ruled cells that share lines (Ramp, Browserbase, Attio).
  • The slide itself as the object, with highlighted quotes (Ramp, Superhuman).
  • Printed labels and a port strip (teenage engineering, Ramp).
  • One loud colour, used only where the lead acts (Ramp).
