WHAT WORKS
The recommendation is clear early and specific. Slide 1: "Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn." The team has built a realistic three-month test structure before full launch that lets the client learn.

WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 1 high impact, 2 moderate

1. The 8% conversion assumption is the main variable the client's decision rests on, but the reasoning for why it is conservative is not shown. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one. The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The client is told the assumption is conservative but cannot see why, so cannot independently assess whether the 8% figure is realistic or defensive. Without that, the client cannot judge whether a month-two result below 8% is a design failure or a market reality. The soft launch is built to test this, but the board needs to understand the logic first.
   What to do: Show the conversion reasoning. Why does a 32% event-attendance rate imply an 8% membership conversion rate should be conservative? If no published comparable exists, state that explicitly and frame the 8% as "our best estimate because—" not "conservative because." Either show the arithmetic or own the assumption as unvalidated.
   Answer first: What is the relationship between attendance and membership conversion for Client E, and what do comparable organizations' conversion rates look like?

2. The soft launch pass threshold of 5% (slide 10) has no stated basis, but it gates whether month three proceeds. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The client needs to know why 5% is the decision boundary for go/no-go. If the soft launch is at 5% and the full launch target is 8%, the client does not know whether 6% at soft scale means the model works or not. This is the month-two decision the board will use.
   What to do: State the rule. Is 5% the break-even point for soft-launch economics? Is it the margin that indicates the full launch is likely to hit 8%? The board needs to understand what month-two data would tell them.
   Answer first: On what basis was the 5% threshold chosen for month two, and what does a result between 5% and 8% mean?

3. The staffing assumption for members-only events is not pre-tested with the soft launch. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 9
   Quote: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff. A soft launch to the most engaged 200 contacts tests both before the full launch."
   Why it costs the client: The brief states there is no budget for new staff and the coordinator is part-time. A soft launch of 200 members produces 2 x 200 = 400 attendee-slots per year at members-only events, on top of existing load. This is not a trivial assumption on existing capacity. If the soft launch strains staff and the team then scales from that baseline in month three, you have the failure baked in before the client can see it.
   What to do: The soft launch should include a staffing reality check. During the 200-member test, do the members-only events stay within the existing weekly hours, or does the coordinator need to expand? Capture that in month two so the board sees both the conversion number and the actual staffing impact at that scale.
   Answer first: How many staff hours do two members-only events per year generate for a 200-member cohort, and does that fit within the coordinator's current capacity?

THE HABIT TO BUILD
The team has a strong habit of naming assumptions and building tests for them. Deepen it: do not just say "conservative" without showing the math. When a number is an assumption, show why you believe it is reasonable. If you have no comparable, say so and own the assumption as unvalidated. That honesty is what turns a soft launch from a feature into a decision gate the client can trust.

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE
Whether a soft launch to the most engaged 200 contacts will be representative of the broader mailing list's conversion likelihood. Whether the pricing is right for Client E's context (three local comparables is a small sample). Whether the coordinator has actually been asked whether this is feasible, or whether the implementation plan has been reviewed with them.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM
1. What membership conversion rates do comparable organizations achieve, and what is our evidence for expecting Client E's to differ?
2. On what basis was the 5% soft launch pass threshold chosen, and what conversion range at soft scale would still recommend a full launch in month three?
3. Has the coordinator been consulted about the feasibility and time cost of running the soft launch plus month-two review while maintaining regular program delivery?
4. The 32% event-attendance figure on slide 4 — is that 32% of the mailing list or 32% of some other group?
5. Do you have comparable organizations' member retention rates, or is the year-one 5,000 pounds the only financial variable you are modeling?
