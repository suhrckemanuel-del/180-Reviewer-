WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 2 high impact, 0 moderate, 0 minor

1. The 8% conversion rate needs a peer benchmark to mean anything to the board. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot assess whether the recommendation is realistic without knowing how 8% compares to actual membership conversion in similar non-profits. Event attendance and membership commitment are different behaviors, so "conservative against 32%" does not tell the board whether to expect 8% or a fraction of it. The board could green-light the recommendation thinking 8% is defensible and later abandon it when soft launch falls short, or walk away from a sound bet.
   What to do: Research what membership conversion looks like in 3-5 comparable non-profits in your region before soft launch. Find the range and where 8% sits in it. If 8% is typical, say so. If it is thin, name what you are betting on instead—what drives conversion in Client E's case. The team's hypothesis about engaged contacts and clear benefits is worth making explicit as your basis.
   Answer first: What do comparable organisations see as membership conversion rates?

2. Staffing assumptions merit explicit testing before the board decides. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 9
   Quote: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff. A soft launch to the most engaged 200 contacts tests both before the full launch."
   Why it costs the client: Client E has one part-time coordinator and no budget for new staff. Slide 7 promises two members-only events a year; Slide 9 assumes this does not strain them. Those two statements together risk hiding the real bottleneck: whether the coordinator can actually deliver. The board may approve membership and later learn the launch stalls for lack of hours. The soft launch tests feasibility, but the team should name staffing as a constraint upfront.
   What to do: Walk the month-by-month calendar with the coordinator: soft launch communications, event production, membership administration, response handling. Be specific about hours. If the plan works only if the coordinator stops something else, name what. If the solution is to outsource one event or automate one task, test it first or budget for it. The board needs to know whether staff is the real risk.
   Answer first: Can the coordinator genuinely absorb two members-only events a year alongside existing work?

THE HABIT TO BUILD

You are good at testing claims instead of just naming them. The soft launch gates are a win. Build this: when a number drives the recommendation, ask what evidence would make a decision-maker trust it. Here, the 8% makes the business case. A board member who has managed memberships elsewhere will have gut intuition about whether 8% passes the smell test. Instead of guessing they will accept it, find the comparator they are actually thinking about. Go one step further every time: not "our assumption is conservative" but "conservative means X percentile in comparable groups."
