# Scope audit, 2026-09-20

Run on the owner's challenge: *"how do we have so many tickets... can we really spin it down and
just do the necessary... I feel like we're doing too much."*

This is an audit, not a decision. Nothing is closed here. Every cut proposed is proposed.

## 1. How the map got to 25 tickets

Charting on 2026-09-18 produced thirteen (01–13). Working them produced twelve more. **The map
roughly doubled by being worked**, and the growth was not evenly spread:

| parent | children it spawned |

| 01, the real-deck baseline | 14, 15, 16, 07's constraints |

| 17, the verification run | 23, and the third prompt version 15 had to choose between |

**Ten of the twelve descendants trace back to one ticket: the baseline run.** And nine of the
twenty-five are about the measuring apparatus rather than the thing being measured: 01, 02, 03, 06,
07, 14, 18, 24, plus half of 16 and 17.

### The specific moment this went wrong

**On 2026-09-19, ticket 14 took the gating eval off the critical path.** The owner's reason is on
the map: the comparisons had not changed a decision, and usability is the priority.

Six tickets have been created since. Five of them are eval work: 17 (verify the eval prediction),
18 (another blocking rule), 23 (a contract defect on the JSON path), 24 (how the eval reaches the
product), and half of 22. **The map kept building the instrument after deciding the instrument was
not on the route.** That is the whole answer to "how do we have so many tickets."

It is worth being precise about what that cost: it was not wasted. Tickets 16 and 17 found the dead
severity floor, the loose blocking rule and the constant confidence label, all real. But they were
found by an instrument that had already been ruled off the critical path, and each finding spawned
more instrument work.

## 4. Even the ship ticket is mostly not pilot work

Ticket 20 has thirteen items. Sorted by whether a pilot user would ever encounter the result:

**Pilot-blocking (4):** fix paste 2's confidence line and record stamp; delete the emitted readiness
level from paste 1; regenerate the stale combined test kit; make the paste artifacts agree.

**Eval apparatus (2):** build the derived scorer, fix the scorecard's unmeasured handling. Useful,
but it serves an eval already off the critical path.

**Out-of-scope artifact (2):** items 2 and 3 edit `index.html` and `site/index.html` — **the hosted
page, which the map explicitly rules out of scope.**

**Documentation (5):** the six-document sweep, the trust-boundary rows.

### The hosted page is the clearest cut on the map

The page is live and, once the readiness retirement lands, it emits something stop condition 3
forbids. That is a real problem with two solutions, and the map has only ever considered one:

- **Edit it** — two files, several selectors, and a page nobody is maintaining toward the product.
- **Take it down** — one action, and the map already says it is not the product and is not
  maintained toward one.

Taking it down removes a live trust-boundary violation, removes two items from ticket 20, and
removes the surface that makes ticket 13 look load-bearing. **Recommended.**

## 5. Ticket 11, reconsidered

The bundle is ~9,400 tokens of instructions and ~13,300 for a typical review (tickets 04 and 11).
That fits comfortably in every mainstream free tier. The real risk is not capacity, it is **silent
truncation**, and that is ticket 12's job.

Ticket 11's prepared session already built the thing that matters: three marker strings at
different depths of the bundle that reveal a silent cut. **That marker design is ticket 12's
answer.** Measuring four tiers by hand then becomes optional, and the tiers will have changed by
the time the pilot runs anyway.

## 6. Ticket 22 is smaller than it looked, and half of it already ships

The owner's words were *"the critical ones are just stacked, why don't we just list them"*.

**The listing already exists.** `CHAT-CONTEXT.md:324` grades every finding
`[High impact | Moderate | Minor]` and the output lists them in priority order. A pilot user
already receives exactly the thing the first half of that sentence asks for.

So ticket 22 reduces to one question: **does a single stacked summary number get added on top?**
And that number is the part that collides with stop condition 3, which was widened eight days ago
to "the tool produces a readiness level, for anyone". A one-question ticket, not a redesign.

## 7. My own two tickets, audited honestly

Both were created today, both pitched as high-value, and the audit does not support that.

**Ticket 24** — the finding is real: the derived scorer structurally cannot read the chat path,
because it needs a blocking rule number the chat path is forbidden to emit. But the consequence
is that **an eval already off the critical path reaches the product even less well than thought.**
That does not block a pilot. The one part that does matter is much smaller and is not a decision:
**nobody has ever read the output this path produces on a real deck.** That is a task worth an
hour, not a grilling ticket.

**Ticket 25** — created to order the frontier; the owner then ordered it by asking this question.
It should close into this audit rather than run as its own session.

**This is the pattern the owner is objecting to, produced twice in one session by the agent that
was warning about it.** Recording it rather than quietly deleting the tickets.
