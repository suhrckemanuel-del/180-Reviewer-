WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The fast pass gives the board the answer, but the first sentence combining the proposed choice with its rationale does not appear until slide 6. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 6
   Quote: "Launching one tier at 45 pounds, rather than three tiers at once, keeps admin low and lets Client E learn before it scales."
   Why it costs the client: A board member reading once must assemble the risk-controlled logic from separate slides, making the recommendation appear more categorical and less conditional than the team intends.
   What to do: Put the governing insight at the start. Make the decision, the capacity constraint and the reason for testing one tier understandable from one sentence before presenting supporting evidence.
   Answer first: What is the single reason the board should choose a one-tier soft launch now?

2. The pilot decision rule is unexplained: the deck sets an 8% target but authorises full launch at 5% without showing why either threshold is sufficient. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: A result between 5% and 8% misses the economic assumption but still triggers launch. The board cannot tell whether that outcome supports scaling, revising the offer or stopping.
   What to do: Distinguish the expected case from the minimum launch threshold. Tie each threshold to net income, workload and a specific proceed, revise or stop decision.
   Answer first: What makes a 5% result sufficient for full launch when the financial case assumes 8%?

3. The central financial case lacks a valid comparator: attendance does not establish paid conversion and £5,000 is not compared with the funding gap, total income or delivery cost. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one. The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot judge whether 8% is plausible or whether £5,000 would materially diversify income. It could approve the scheme based on upside that sounds precise but remains uninterpretable.
   What to do: Build a conversion range from relevant evidence or label it as an unvalidated scenario. Compare net proceeds with the funding gap, the client's diversification objective and the minimum viable result.
   Answer first: What paid conversion evidence supports 8%, and what annual net contribution would make membership worthwhile?

4. Feasibility is asserted rather than tested against the one part-time coordinator constraint. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 7
   Quote: "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners. These use capacity Client E already has."
   Why it costs the client: The board could approve benefits whose administration, event delivery and partner management consume more capacity than the resulting income justifies.
   What to do: Estimate coordinator hours by activity, identify event and membership capacity limits and define how the offer changes if the pilot exceeds those limits.
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours would the pilot and full scheme require during launch and normal operation?

THE HABIT TO BUILD

Treat every recommendation-driving number as a decision rule. The weak habit is presenting a plausible magnitude before establishing its benchmark and implication. For the next deck, complete a three-column exercise for each central figure: comparator, meaning and resulting client action.
