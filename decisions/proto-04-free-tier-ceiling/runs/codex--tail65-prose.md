Strengths

The deck makes a clear recommendation, uses a staged launch and assigns ownership. It also identifies conversion and staff capacity as assumptions rather than presenting them as facts. The strongest implementation sentence is: “The Client E coordinator owns the launch. Month one: soft launch to 200 contacts.” (Slide 10)

Priority findings

1. Major | communication | deliveryCritical: false

Diagnosis: The governing insight is absent under the single-sentence test. The action appears in “Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn.” (Slide 1), while the reason appears separately in “Project grants are 80% of Client E's income (2024 accounts) and each is single-year.” (Slide 3). A title-and-first-sentence skim therefore gives the recommendation and supporting topics, but not one explicit action-and-reason conclusion.

Why: The board must assemble the case itself before evaluating the recommendation. This weakens an otherwise clear argument but does not obscure the proposed action.

Fix: Make the opening governing insight connect the recommended small launch directly to grant concentration and the need to test uncertain demand. Keep the rest of the storyline as proof of that conclusion.

2. Major | thinking | deliveryCritical: true

Diagnosis: The deck does not show whether the projected income would materially diversify Client E's finances. It states, “If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one.” (Slide 8), but never compares £5,000 with total income, the value of the grants at risk or a board-defined diversification target.

Why: The board cannot tell whether membership addresses the stated income problem or merely creates additional work for an immaterial contribution.

Fix: Compare the projected net contribution across conversion scenarios with the income at risk and an explicit diversification objective. Let that comparison determine whether membership is worthwhile.

3. Critical | evidence | deliveryCritical: true

Diagnosis: The go or no-go rule rests on two unexplained conversion figures. The deck calls 8% “conservative against the 32% attendance rate” (Slide 8), although attendance is not established as a membership-conversion benchmark. It then says, “Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%.” (Slide 10), without explaining why 5% justifies scaling.

Why: The board could authorise a full launch at 5% even if that result fails the stated target and produces too little income to justify the workload.

Fix: Treat 8% as a scenario rather than a defensible forecast unless relevant conversion evidence exists. Derive one continuation threshold from the minimum acceptable net income, workload and learning objective, then show sensitivity above and below it.

4. Major | implementation | deliveryCritical: true

Diagnosis: Capacity is asserted and assumed rather than demonstrated. Slide 7 says, “These use capacity Client E already has.” Slide 9 says, “We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff.” The proposed soft launch tests sign-up activity, but the deck does not show how it will test the workload of two annual events, partner discounts and ongoing member administration before expansion.

Why: Client E could scale a scheme that the part-time coordinator cannot fulfil consistently, putting both member experience and existing work at risk.

Fix: Map the recurring tasks and hours required at pilot and full scale. Design the pilot to test fulfilment workload as well as conversion, with a capacity threshold in the expansion decision.

Questions for the lead

- What are the numerator and denominator behind the “32% event-attendance rate” on Slide 4?
- What minimum net income or reduction in grant dependence would make membership worthwhile to the board?
- How will the soft launch test event delivery, partner administration and ongoing servicing before the month-three decision?

Learning note

Reusable habit: derive assumptions and thresholds from the client's decision criteria rather than choosing plausible percentages first.

Weak habit: the deck moves from an engagement statistic to an 8% forecast and then to a separate 5% expansion threshold without an economic bridge.

Exercise: build a one-page decision table showing conversion, gross income, delivery cost, coordinator hours and net contribution at 5%, 8% and a downside case. Use the minimum acceptable outcome to set the continuation rule.
