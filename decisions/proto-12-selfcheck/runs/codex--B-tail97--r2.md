WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The 8% conversion assumption is treated as conservative, but the deck does not give the board a like-for-like comparator for membership conversion. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The board could mistake event attendance as evidence of willingness to pay for membership. That affects whether they approve a full launch, change the soft-launch threshold or ask for a lower-risk test first.
   What to do: Separate engagement evidence from conversion evidence. Keep the 8% as an explicit test assumption, then add the best available comparator or explain why none exists and what result would make the assumption acceptable.
   Answer first: What would make 8% credible enough for this board: peer membership conversion, past donation behaviour, survey intent, soft-launch response or another benchmark?

2. The member benefits are assumed to fit current capacity, but the deck does not show the operational load behind that claim. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 7
   Quote: "These use capacity Client E already has."
   Why it costs the client: The recommendation depends on one part-time coordinator being able to run the scheme without new staff. If the benefits create hidden admin or event work, the board may approve a plan the organisation cannot execute.
   What to do: Show a minimum operating model for the launch. Break the benefits into concrete tasks, estimate who does them and flag which benefit should be cut first if capacity is tighter than assumed.
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours are required in month one, month two and steady state?

3. The risk section names low conversion, but it does not state the financial downside if the soft launch underperforms. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 9
   Quote: "The main risk is low conversion."
   Why it costs the client: The board needs to know whether failure means a small learning cost or a material distraction from grant work. Without the downside, it cannot judge whether the pilot is proportionate.
   What to do: Add the downside case behind the pilot. State the lowest plausible conversion outcome, what revenue it produces, what effort it consumes and the stop or adapt decision it triggers.
   Answer first: If the 200-contact soft launch clears 0%, 3% or 5%, what exactly should the board do next?
