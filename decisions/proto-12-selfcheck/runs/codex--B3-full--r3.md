WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The deck gives two different success thresholds for the same soft launch decision. [High impact]
   Where: Slides 8 and 10
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot tell whether the pilot succeeds at 5% or 8%. That changes whether they scale after 10 members from 200 contacts or wait for roughly 16, which is the actual go or no-go decision.
   What to do: Make one decision rule govern the pilot. If 5% is the minimum viable threshold and 8% is the year-one planning case, separate those roles and explain why both numbers matter.
   Answer first: What exact conversion rate makes the board proceed to full launch, and what exact rate makes them stop or redesign?

2. The 8% conversion assumption is not yet defended by a true comparator. [High impact]
   Where: Slides 4 and 8
   Quote: "Client E has 1,400 people on its mailing list and a 32% event-attendance rate (box-office data, 2024)."
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one."
   Why it costs the client: Attendance shows engagement but not willingness to pay for membership. Without a conversion benchmark, prior campaign result or sensitivity range, the board cannot judge whether 5,000 pounds is a prudent plan or an optimistic case.
   What to do: Treat 8% as an explicit scenario, not a proven forecast. Add the comparator that would settle it: past Client E donor conversion, comparable membership uptake or a sensitivity table around lower conversion rates.
   Answer first: What evidence makes 8% a reasonable planning case for paid membership rather than only a hypothesis to test?

3. The benefits package is named, but the staffing burden is not tested against the known capacity constraint. [Moderate]
   Where: Slides 7 and 9
   Quote: "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners."
   Quote: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
   Why it costs the client: The recommendation depends on existing capacity, but the deck does not show what the coordinator must actually do to deliver benefits. The board could approve a scheme whose admin load undermines the low-risk design.
   What to do: Add a simple workload check for the coordinator: setup, communications, bookings, partner coordination, event delivery and review. Keep it directional, but make the operational burden visible.
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours does the soft launch and full launch require, and which activities can be absorbed without dropping current work?

4. The risk section names low conversion but does not say what Client E should do if the test fails. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 9
   Quote: "The main risk is low conversion."
   Quote: "A soft launch to the most engaged 200 contacts tests both before the full launch."
   Why it costs the client: A test only reduces risk if the board knows how to act on the result. Without a response plan, a weak pilot may still drift into full launch because the next decision is not specified.
   What to do: Define the actions for each result band: proceed, revise the offer, retest with a different segment or stop. Keep the decision tree light but make failure usable.
   Answer first: If the soft launch converts below the threshold, what should Client E change before asking the wider list?
