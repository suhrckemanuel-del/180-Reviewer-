WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The soft-launch decision rule gives the board two different thresholds for success. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot tell whether the pilot has to prove 8% conversion or only 5% before committing to a full launch. That changes whether they scale, adjust the offer or stop.
   What to do: Choose one decision threshold or explain the role of each threshold. The board needs a single go, adapt or stop rule before launch.
   Answer first: What conversion result from 200 contacts would make Client E confidently continue, and why is that enough?

2. The 8% assumption is named as conservative, but the comparator does not prove conversion. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: Attendance shows engagement, not willingness to pay for membership. If 8% is too high, the expected 5,000 pounds may overstate the income case the board is being asked to approve.
   What to do: Separate engagement evidence from conversion evidence. Keep 8% as a testable planning assumption unless the team can add a more relevant benchmark or sensitivity range.
   Answer first: Compared with what actual membership conversion benchmark, past campaign result or sensitivity case is 8% conservative?

3. The capacity risk is stated, but the deck does not show whether the benefits fit one part-time coordinator. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 7
   Quote: "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners. These use capacity Client E already has."
   Why it costs the client: The board may approve an offer that creates hidden work: partner coordination, event planning, booking administration and member communication. That matters because the known constraint is existing capacity only.
   What to do: Add a light workload test for each benefit. Keep only benefits that can be delivered with named existing processes, or mark the benefit as a later-stage option.
   Answer first: Which specific existing activity, system or calendar slot absorbs each member benefit without adding staff hours?
