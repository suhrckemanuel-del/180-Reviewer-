WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The 8% conversion assumption drives the recommendation but is not benchmarked against a membership conversion comparator. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one."
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot tell whether 5,000 pounds is a realistic upside or an optimistic case dressed as a base case, so the income decision rests on a number whose meaning is not yet settled.
   What to do: Separate the arithmetic from the confidence level. Show why 8% is the right planning case, what happens at lower conversion levels and which result would still justify launch.
   Answer first: What evidence would make the board believe 8% is a prudent planning assumption rather than just the team’s chosen scenario?

2. The deck compares membership conversion to attendance, but it does not explain why attendance is the right reference point. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: Attendance proves engagement, not willingness to pay for membership. Without the bridge between those behaviours, the board may over-read demand and approve a launch target the audience will not meet.
   What to do: Add the mechanism that links attendance to paid membership. If the link is uncertain, say that directly and use the soft launch as validation rather than proof.
   Answer first: Why should an event attendee be expected to behave like a prospective member?

3. The launch threshold is internally unclear: the deck says to review against 8% but proceed if the soft launch clears 5%. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The board does not know whether success means reaching the revenue case or merely clearing a lower test threshold, and those lead to different go or pause decisions after month two.
   What to do: Define the decision rule. Make one threshold the launch gate, explain why it is sufficient and show what action follows if conversion lands between 5% and 8%.
   Answer first: If the soft launch converts 6%, should Client E launch fully, adjust the offer or stop?

4. The capacity claim is plausible but not yet shown, especially for members-only events. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 7
   Quote: "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners. These use capacity Client E already has."
   Why it costs the client: The recommendation depends on not adding operational burden. If the two events create extra planning work for a part-time coordinator, the scheme may diversify income while worsening delivery strain.
   What to do: Add a light capacity check for each benefit. Distinguish benefits that are operationally free from those that need staff time, partner coordination or event delivery.
   Answer first: Which specific tasks does the coordinator need to do to deliver the two members-only events, and how many hours will they take?
