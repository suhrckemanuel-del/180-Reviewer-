WHAT WORKS

1. The soft launch is a concrete learning mechanism that tests the two biggest unknowns before full commitment. "Month one: soft launch to 200 contacts. Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%." (Slide 10). This is proportionate to the constraint and risk profile.

2. The recommendation acknowledges operational limits rather than ignoring them. "Launching one tier at 45 pounds, rather than three tiers at once, keeps admin low and lets Client E learn before it scales." (Slide 6). A single tier with existing benefits is honest about what one part-time coordinator can sustain.

3. The team is transparent about what they do not know. "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test." (Slide 8). Flagging the key assumption rather than burying it builds trust in the analysis.

WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The revenue impact does not clearly address the problem statement [High impact]
   Where: Slides 3 and 8
   Quote: "Two of five grants are not expected to renew, per the funder letters on file." (Slide 3) and "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one." (Slide 8)
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot evaluate whether membership revenue offsets the grant loss without knowing what value the two at-risk grants represent. Without this comparison, the recommendation's scale and sufficiency are unclear.
   What to do: Calculate the annual value of the two at-risk grants. Compare that amount to the projected 5,000 pounds in membership revenue. If membership is insufficient to replace the loss, frame it as a partial solution. If it matches or exceeds the loss, say so explicitly.
   Answer first: What is the annual value of the two grants at risk, and how does it compare to the membership projection?

2. The 8% conversion assumption lacks adequate grounding [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: Comparing free event attendance to paid membership conversion mixes different value propositions. The comparables (40-60 pounds pricing) are mentioned but never connected to the conversion logic. Without this bridge, the board has no basis to trust the number.
   What to do: Show the reasoning that connects attendance to conversion. Either reference a comparable organization's conversion rate, or explain why 8% is plausible given the specific benefits offered (priority booking, two events, partner discounts) and the known price point.
   Answer first: What is the conversion pathway from current audience engagement to membership uptake, and what evidence supports 8% as achievable?

3. Delivering two members-only events per year within existing capacity is not evidenced [High impact]
   Where: Slide 7
   Quote: "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners. These use capacity Client E already has."
   Why it costs the client: Given one part-time coordinator and no new budget, adding two annual events is a commitment. Without showing how this fits into the current schedule, the board cannot evaluate whether the benefit promise is viable.
   What to do: Map the current annual event schedule and volunteer load. Show where the two new events fit. If they require changes to existing programming or staff patterns, name that.
   Answer first: What is the current event capacity and schedule, and where do the two new member events fit into it?

THE HABIT TO BUILD

The team shows good instinct: they test assumptions (soft launch), design for constraints (single tier), and flag uncertainty (8% flagged as "main thing to test"). The weak habit: assumptions are named but not grounded in external data or reasoning before being presented to the board. Every number that drives a decision should have a source or a credible comparison. Exercise: for the next recommendation, identify the three numbers the decision hinges on. For each, find one comparator (a peer's rate, a prior period, a market benchmark, or a first-principles calculation). Show the reasoning in the deck.

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE

- Whether 8% conversion is realistic for this audience (no domain knowledge of arts membership or access to sector benchmarks)
- Whether two additional events per year are actually feasible given the unspecified current event load and volunteer support
- Whether the soft launch threshold of 5% is appropriate for triggering full launch, or whether it should be higher or lower
- Whether the benefit mix (priority booking, events, partner discounts) is actually appealing to the core audience (would require user research or peer feedback)

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM

1. What is the annual value of the two grants at risk, and how does it compare to projected membership revenue?
2. What specific conversion rates, if any, have comparable arts organizations in your region achieved with membership launches at similar price points?
3. How certain is the accuracy of the 1,400-contact mailing list? Has the list been cleaned or verified recently?
4. What is the current number and frequency of events Client E runs annually, and what would adding two member-only events require operationally?
5. If the soft launch reaches only 3% conversion instead of 5%, would that be sufficient to proceed with full launch, or is 5% a true floor for the decision?
