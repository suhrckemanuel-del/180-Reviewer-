WHAT WORKS

2. The deck gives the board a concrete first operating sequence rather than only a concept. Slide 10 says: "Month one: soft launch to 200 contacts. Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."

3. The team states the main risk directly instead of hiding it. Slide 9 says: "The main risk is low conversion."

WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The revenue case is not yet interpretable because the deck does not compare 5,000 pounds to the funding gap or the income diversification target. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one."
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot tell whether membership is a meaningful diversification move or a small experiment worth doing only for learning. That changes how much attention, risk tolerance and coordinator time they should allocate.
   What to do: Add the missing comparator. Size the year-one membership upside against the lost or at-risk grant income, total income or a target diversification threshold, then state what 5,000 pounds does and does not solve.
   Answer first: What financial problem is membership meant to solve in year one: replacing lost grant income, reducing concentration risk or proving future potential?

2. The 8% conversion assumption is presented as conservative, but the quoted comparator does not prove that membership conversion should track event attendance. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The board may treat the forecast as more grounded than it is. Attendance shows engagement, but it does not by itself establish willingness to pay for annual membership.
   What to do: Reframe 8% as an explicit test assumption unless you can support it with a closer benchmark. Explain why attendance is only directional evidence and show the result range if conversion is lower.
   Answer first: What evidence would make 8% credible enough for a board decision: past donor behavior, comparable membership conversion or soft-launch precommitments?

3. The risk section names staff strain but does not show the operating load created by the promised benefits. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 7
   Quote: "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners. These use capacity Client E already has."
   Why it costs the client: The board could approve a scheme that is financially modest but operationally distracting. With one part-time coordinator, the risk is not only conversion but whether the offer can be run without crowding out core work.
   What to do: Turn the capacity claim into a simple operating check. Estimate the coordinator tasks, partner management, event workload and admin moments created by the membership tier, then make the launch scope match that capacity.
   Answer first: What must the coordinator actually do each month to deliver the membership promise?

4. The recommendation has a built-in threshold conflict: month two reviews against 8%, but month three proceeds if the soft launch clears 5%. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The board will not know whether 5% is a warning sign, a minimum viable result or a success case. That weakens governance at exactly the decision point the pilot is meant to create.
   What to do: Define the decision rule. Separate the target, the minimum launch threshold and the stop or redesign trigger, then explain what management action follows each outcome.
   Answer first: If the soft launch converts 5% but not 8%, what should Client E actually do?

Exercise: for each number in the deck, write three short answers beside it before editing the slide: compared to what, good or bad, and what action follows.
