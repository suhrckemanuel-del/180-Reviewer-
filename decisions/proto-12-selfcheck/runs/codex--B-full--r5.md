WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The launch gate conflicts with the target the client is told to test. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The board could approve a full launch after a result that the deck itself frames as below target. At 5% to 7.9%, the client needs to know whether to proceed, adjust the offer or stop.
   What to do: Make the decision rule single and explicit. Separate the economic target from any minimum learning threshold if both are needed.
   Answer first: What result from the 200-contact soft launch would make the board say yes to full launch, and why?

2. The 8% conversion assumption is not yet supported by the right comparator. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: Attendance rate does not directly show willingness to pay for membership. If 8% is too high, the income case and the month-two test both overstate what the scheme can deliver.
   What to do: Treat 8% as an explicit scenario assumption unless you can anchor it in comparable membership conversion, Client E donor behavior or a tested expression of willingness to pay.
   Answer first: What evidence would make 8% a realistic paid-membership conversion rate rather than a plausible internal planning assumption?

3. The capacity risk is named but not stress-tested against the benefits offered. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 7
   Quote: "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners. These use capacity Client E already has."
   Why it costs the client: The client has one part-time coordinator, so the board needs to see whether the offer can be delivered without crowding out grant delivery, programming or partner management.
   What to do: Add a small operating check: tasks, owner time, partner dependency and what gets dropped if conversion is higher or lower than expected.
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours and partner actions does the proposed member offer require in the first three months?

THE HABIT TO BUILD
Separate a test metric from a decision gate. The weak habit is naming a sensible pilot but leaving the rule for acting on it ambiguous. Exercise: for every recommendation, write "If the measured result is X, the client does Y because Z" before building the slide.
