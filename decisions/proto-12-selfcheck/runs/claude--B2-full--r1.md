WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The 8% conversion assumption needs grounding and the 5,000 pound revenue needs context [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one. The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The board will ask two questions. First: why is 8% realistic or conservative, given that only 32% of the mailing list attends events? The deck asserts a link between these numbers but does not explain it. Second: is 5,000 pounds material for Client E? Is it enough to matter for income diversification, or is it too small to justify the effort? Without answers, the board cannot assess whether membership is worth pursuing, even as a test. This directly affects whether they greenlight the soft launch.
   What to do: Ground the 8% with one of three approaches: show benchmark conversion rates for membership in similar organizations and explain how 8% compares (conservative, aggressive, middle of the road); explain the behavioral funnel (of people who attend events, what % do you expect to convert to paid membership and why); or explicitly acknowledge it is an unsourced estimate and emphasize that testing is the entire point, then clarify what result at soft launch would trigger full launch. For the 5,000 pounds: show it as a percentage of Client E's 2024 total income, and net it against any membership program costs. This gives the board a sense of magnitude and progress toward reducing grant dependency.
   Answer first: What benchmark data exists for membership conversion in similar arts organizations, or what is the behavioral rationale for expecting 8% of the mailing list to convert when 32% attend events?

THE HABIT TO BUILD
The team demonstrates good risk management in the soft launch approach, which is genuinely a strong habit. The weak habit is grounding financial cases with comparative context. When a recommendation rests on a number, show three things: what you are comparing it to (benchmarks, prior results, comparable organizations), why that baseline matters (it is conservative, it is peer-validated, it is from a test), and what happens if you are wrong (here, the 5% soft-launch gate is that answer). Exercise: before you lock a financial projection, first document what three comparable cases show, then write one sentence explaining why your assumption is conservative or bold relative to them. That sentence should be in the deck.

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE
- Whether 8% conversion is realistic for this client without access to published data on membership program conversion rates in similar-sized arts non-profits.
- Whether 5,000 pounds is material for this client's annual budget (the 2024 accounts are referenced but not disclosed in the deliverable).
- Whether the coordinator can realistically manage the membership program given their other duties without the deck providing detail on available capacity.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM
1. What conversion rates do published case studies show for membership programs in arts organizations of similar size?
2. What is Client E's total 2024 income, and what are the reasonable direct costs of running membership in year one (platform fees, staff time, events)?
3. If the soft launch converts at 5% rather than 8%, is the program still worth a full launch, and does the math still support diversification?
4. Why should membership conversion (percentage of mailing list paying money) track with event attendance rates (percentage of mailing list attending in person)?
