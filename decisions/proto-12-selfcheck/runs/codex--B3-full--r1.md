WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The 8% conversion assumption is doing too much work without the right comparator. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The board may treat the year-one income as more defensible than it is. Event attendance proves engagement, but it does not show willingness to pay for membership.
   What to do: Reframe 8% as a test assumption, not as conservative unless you can benchmark paid membership conversion. Show the break-even or useful range at lower conversion levels.
   Answer first: What conversion rate would still make this worth launching for Client E?

2. The launch threshold conflicts with the target, so the board cannot tell what result means proceed. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: Client E could full-launch after a result that misses the stated target. That changes the decision from a disciplined test to a softer go-ahead.
   What to do: Separate the success target from the minimum go/no-go threshold. Explain what happens below 5%, between 5% and 8% and above 8%.
   Answer first: Is 5% a minimum viable threshold, or is 8% the condition for full launch?

3. The capacity risk is asserted as solved rather than tested. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 7
   Quote: "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners. These use capacity Client E already has."
   Why it costs the client: The recommended benefit package may create hidden staff work for a one-coordinator organisation, especially events and partner discounts.
   What to do: Show the operating load for each benefit and move any unproven benefit into the soft launch test. Keep the first offer to what the coordinator can actually deliver.
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours will each member benefit require in the first three months?

4. The risk section names the key risk, but it does not state what Client E will do if the test fails. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 9
   Quote: "The main risk is low conversion."
   Why it costs the client: A board can agree to test, but still lack a decision rule for stopping, changing the offer or delaying the full launch.
   What to do: Add response actions for failed, mixed and successful soft-launch outcomes. Make the risk plan operational, not just descriptive.
   Answer first: If the 200-contact soft launch converts below 5%, what exactly changes?
