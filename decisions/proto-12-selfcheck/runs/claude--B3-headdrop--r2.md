WHAT WORKS

The recommendation answers the client's question directly and commits to a specific decision path. "Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn" (Slide 1) is clear, bounded and owned. The team chose a launch date, a price point, a delivery model, and a decision gate rather than handing the client multiple scenarios.

The soft launch as a structured test is strong. Rather than launching at full scale and hoping, the team stages the commitment: 200 contacts first, measure against a 5% floor, then full launch if it clears that bar. This is exactly what a part-time-coordinated non-profit can execute and learn from.

WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The conversion assumption lacks grounding and the soft launch decision rule is incomplete. [High impact]
   Where: Slides 8, 9, 10
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one. The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test." / "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The entire financial case and go-no-go decision rests on two numbers with no supporting evidence. The client cannot evaluate whether the 5% floor is ambitious or lenient, or what it means if the soft launch returns 6.5%. The decision logic is incomplete. If the team reaches 5% with 200 people, what does that imply for full launch? The client needs a clear rule: at what result do we pivot, pause or stop?
   What to do: Research conversion rates from comparable membership schemes serving similar audiences. State the assumption explicitly: "Comparable schemes report conversion of X% to Y%; we selected 8% as a conservative entry point for Client E." Then address the soft launch rule: what is the team's threshold for each outcome? Draft language for what happens if results land in three zones (well below, around, well above the 5% mark). The goal is not certainty; it is a shared understanding of what the test will tell you.
   Answer first: What membership conversion rates have you found from comparable schemes, and how do those drive your 8% choice? If a 200-person soft launch reaches 5%, what does the team recommend that Client E does next?

2. The risk section names assumptions but does not square them with reality. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 9
   Quote: "The main risk is low conversion. We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
   Why it costs the client: The team names two risks (mailing list quality and staff capacity) but does not tell the client what low conversion would actually look like operationally. If 2% join instead of 8%, does the scheme still launch? Is there a revenue floor? The staff capacity risk is real and serious for a one-person operation; the slide assumes it away rather than testing it. These are not secondary refinements; they are constraints that could change the recommendation itself.
   What to do: Separate what Client E can test before launch (mailing list accuracy: do bounce tests, soft launch confirmation of engagement) from what the soft launch will reveal (staff capacity, member satisfaction). For each risk, name the consequence if it proves real: low engagement might mean lower revenue than modelled; high staff load might mean cutting the events or running them simpler. Show that you have thought through what each risk means for the launch decision.
   Answer first: If the mailing list quality is poor (high bounce rate) or if running two events would require more than the coordinator can absorb, what changes about the recommendation?

3. The comparable membership analysis stops at price. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 5
   Quote: "Three comparable local arts non-profits run membership at 40 to 60 pounds a year (their public websites, 2025). Client E has no membership today."
   Why it costs the client: Knowing the price range is valuable. Not knowing whether those schemes are growing or shrinking, who actually joins, or what the retention looks like after year one leaves Client E without the outside view. The client deserves to know: are these schemes working? What are they teaching you about what Client E should copy or avoid?
   What to do: For each comparable scheme, add one piece of evidence about how it is performing: rough member count, whether it is growing year-over-year if you can find it, or a sentence about what you learned from looking at their benefits structure. If you cannot find this, say plainly that you have the price but not the health, and flag that as a soft launch question: call one of these schemes and ask what their conversion and retention look like.
   Answer first: Beyond the price, what do the three comparable schemes tell you about whether membership works for small arts non-profits?

4. The benefits package is stated but not tied to audience demand. [Minor]
   Where: Slide 7
   Quote: "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners. These use capacity Client E already has."
   Why it costs the client: The benefits are real and the team did connect them to available capacity, which is smart. The missing piece is evidence that the audience actually values these things. People who attend 32% of Client E's events might not care about priority booking; they might care about a discount on future events or a gift. The soft launch will test this, so flag it as a learning question rather than a defect, but show that you have thought about whether these benefits will move people to pay.
   What to do: State the assumption: "We chose these benefits because they use existing capacity, but Client E's soft launch will show whether they are actually valuable to the audience. We should be ready to add or swap benefits based on feedback." This is honest and it sets up the soft launch as the test it needs to be.
   Answer first: Why did you select these three specific benefits over others Client E could offer?

THE HABIT TO BUILD

The habit is the difference between naming an assumption and testing an assumption. You have named several (mailing list accuracy, event capacity, the 8% conversion). The next level is building a test into the plan for each one. The soft launch as currently structured tests one thing: conversion on a subset. It should test three: how many real conversions, how much staff load, and whether the benefits package actually attracts people. Design each test explicitly.

The exercise: list every assumption in the deck. For each, write one sentence about how the soft launch or a quick pre-launch check will confirm or disprove it. If you cannot test it, that is important; say so and explain what you will do instead.

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE

- Whether membership conversion in the UK arts non-profit sector actually clusters around 8% for paid offerings at this price point.
- Whether Client E's audience (defined here as people on the mailing list and event attendees) will genuinely prefer the stated benefits over other possible membership perks.
- Whether the soft launch audience of 200 is large enough to reach statistical confidence in the 5% conversion floor.
- Whether the operating calendar in autumn is actually a realistic launch window given other fundraising or programming Client E has planned.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM

1. Beyond price, what did you learn from looking at those three comparable schemes about what works and what does not in their membership models?
2. The soft launch reaches 5% on 200 people, which is 10 conversions. Do you have confidence in that as a floor, or should it be higher given the conversion you need for full launch?
3. Have you checked whether the autumn launch window works with the coordinator's actual calendar, or is that also an assumption the soft launch will test?
4. You are assuming the mailing list is accurate. What is your plan if it turns out to be stale? Do you have a backup audience for the soft launch?
5. When you say "a discount with local partners," do you have those partnerships secured, or is that one more assumption for the soft launch to validate?
