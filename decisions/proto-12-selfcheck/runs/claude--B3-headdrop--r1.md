WHAT WORKS

The recommendation is clear and actionable. Slide 1 states the decision plainly: "Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn." This is the answer the client asked for, and the team commits to it. The soft launch approach in Slides 9 and 10 is genuinely wise: testing conversion against the 8% assumption on the most engaged 200 contacts before full launch is the concrete first step a small team can actually execute.

The narrative arc from problem to solution tracks cleanly. Slides 3 through 5 establish the income problem and the audience ready to convert, then Slides 6 and 7 show how a single tier keeps the workload realistic. That progression is easy to follow and the reasoning is visible.

WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The 8% conversion assumption is stated but never shown to be defensible. [High impact]
   Where: Slides 8 and 9
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one. The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The recommendation and the financial case rest entirely on this number. The client needs to know what gives the team confidence in 8%, not just that it is "conservative against" the attendance rate. A 32% attendance rate does not imply any specific conversion rate for a paid offering. Without a comparator or a worked case, the client cannot evaluate whether this is a reasonable number to bet on, and if the soft launch clears 5% instead, there is no framework for deciding what that means.
   What to do: Show the reasoning: what comparable schemes actually convert at, what Client E's own engagement data suggests (event attendees who buy vs. who don't), or a worked case from one comparable group. This is not about changing the number; it is about showing the client what the team saw that led to 8%. State what the soft launch really tests: is 8% the right bar or should it move?
   Answer first: What data points, from Client E's own records or from comparable groups, support the 8% figure as reasonable rather than conservative?

2. The comparator on the membership price is incomplete. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 5
   Quote: "Three comparable local arts non-profits run membership at 40 to 60 pounds a year (their public websites, 2025). Client E has no membership today."
   Why it costs the client: The price lands inside the range, which is good, but the slide stops there. The client needs to know whether these comparable schemes succeed at these price points, whether they target the same audience and capacity, and what they actually deliver for the membership fee. A price in range is not a reason on its own; it is only the floor.
   What to do: Name what makes this price defensible: do the comparable schemes reach similar audiences, do they deliver similar value for the tier, do they report good retention or conversion. If you cannot source that, say that the price is data-driven on market range alone and that Client E's own soft launch will be the real test.
   Answer first: For each of the three comparable schemes, what is their membership price, who joins, and what do members report about the value they get?

3. The feasibility claim about members-only events is not evidenced. [Moderate]
   Where: Slides 7 and 9
   Quote: "Two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners. These use capacity Client E already has." / "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
   Why it costs the client: Running two member events on top of the existing schedule is an assumption hidden inside a phrase. The client and the team need to surface what "capacity Client E already has" means operationally. One part-time coordinator will run the membership launch (Slide 10). If those two events are new workload, they matter. If they are already scheduled time reconfigured, that changes everything. Right now it is unclear, and the coordinator is the constraint.
   What to do: Name the two events explicitly: what time of year, what format, how they fit into the coordinator's actual calendar. Show whether they require new hours or just a reshaping of existing work. This is the check the soft launch should answer, so name that here as an assumption to validate.
   Answer first: In the coordinator's actual year, what is the current capacity for events, and does adding "two members-only events a year" require new hours or not?

4. The soft launch target is underspecified as a decision rule. [Moderate]
   Where: Slides 9 and 10
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The soft launch tests the 8% assumption by hitting only 5%. This is the team's way of building in margin; that is fine. But the client does not know what happens if the soft launch returns 6% or 7%, and there is no decision rule. If it returns 3%, does the team abandon the scheme? Pause? Pivot? The client needs clarity on this before launch.
   What to do: Name explicitly what the decision rule is: if soft launch reaches X%, go to full launch; if it reaches Y%, pause and gather more data; if it is below Z%, abandon or restart. You do not need to fill in the numbers yet, but state the logic. This is one of the hardest decisions in implementation and it belongs in the plan, not in the follow-up.
   Answer first: What does Client E do if the soft launch returns 3%, 5%, 7%, or 10%? Is there a number below which the scheme stops?

THE HABIT TO BUILD

The habit is separating "I compared this to something" from "I explained why that comparison settles the question." You have comparators for price and attendance, which is excellent. The next step is always: so what? Does this comparable scheme work? Is Client E's situation like theirs? A comparison without the implication is still work by the team, but it is work the client has to redo. Show the client what the comparison tells them, or say plainly that you do not have the data yet.

The exercise: take Slides 5 and 8 and rewrite them by starting with what Client E needs to decide about each number. Then add only the evidence that moves that decision. Cut everything that just proves you looked it up.

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE

- Whether 8% is reasonable as a starting assumption in membership conversion for small arts non-profits. The rule of thumb across sectors varies widely and I do not have sector knowledge here.
- Whether the described membership benefits (priority booking, two events, partner discounts) are actually valuable to Client E's audience. This depends on what the audience cares about, which only Client E knows.
- Whether the price point would change if comparable schemes turned out to deliver much more or much less for the same fee.
- Whether the coordinator can realistically absorb a soft launch in autumn given Client E's other deadlines and events.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM

1. When you say the 8% assumption is "conservative against the 32% attendance rate," what is the explicit reasoning? Did you test this on a comparable group or is it a judgment call?
2. You found three comparable schemes at 40 to 60 pounds. Did you look at any data on how many people joined those schemes or what happened after the first year?
3. The soft launch tests conversion, but what is the smallest soft launch result that would trigger a pivot rather than a full launch? Is there a number below which the scheme stops?
4. In a typical month right now, how much time does the coordinator spend on member-facing activities, and how much would the two members-only events add to that?
5. The recommendation assumes the mailing list is accurate. Do you have a sense of how stale it might be? Has Client E cleaned it recently?
