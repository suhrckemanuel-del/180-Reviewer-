WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 2 high impact, 2 moderate

1. The soft launch has no operational roadmap [High impact]
   Slide 10 - "Month one: soft launch to 200 contacts."
   The problem: The deck names the soft launch but not how it works. What is the offer? Through which channel (email, call, letter)? What does the signup process look like? When does payment processing begin? The coordinator cannot act on "soft launch" alone.
   Why it matters: The coordinator has one month to execute and cannot spend hours reverse-engineering what "soft launch" means. Without a worked example of the offer or the signup flow, the client is left to assemble instructions the team should give. The month-two review measures execution of a plan the client had to guess at, not the team's actual recommendation.
   Do this: Sequence the soft launch concretely: which channel, what the offer message says (draft or template), how signup works, when the coordinator would expect payments. You do not build a live landing page. You show what it looks like and what the coordinator does to set it up.
   Aim for a line like: If the team sends the 200 a one-paragraph offer via email with a link to a one-page signup form, the coordinator can scope the work in hours and can launch within one week.
   Answer first: What is the exact sequence of steps the coordinator takes to launch the soft offer, starting from day one of month one?

2. The 8% conversion has no market anchor [High impact]
   Slide 8 - "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   The problem: The deck calls 8% an assumption and says it is "conservative" but doesn't establish why. A 32% event attendance rate does not directly compare to an 8% annual membership conversion. Many people attend an event who will not pay an annual subscription, and the comparison doesn't account for that difference.
   Why it matters: The 8% drives the year-one revenue target (5,000 pounds). If the client approves based on this figure and true uptake is 3%, year-one revenue is 1,800 pounds instead of 5,000 — still positive but a different decision on whether membership is worthwhile as diversification. The soft launch tests this at a 5% threshold, which is sound. But the team should be able to tell the client why 8% is plausible before the soft launch, not just conservative.
   Do this: Find or build a comparator for the 8% figure. Benchmark 8% conversion against actual uptake from the three comparable arts groups if that data is available. If not, state what the team would expect from the soft launch and explain why 8% is the next step from there.
   Answer first: What is a realistic first-month conversion rate for a soft launch to most-engaged contacts, and what does Client E's 32% attendance tell us about the jump from there to 8% annual membership?

3. The price point has no strategic grounding [Moderate]
   Slide 5 and 6 - "Three comparable local arts non-profits run membership at 40 to 60 pounds a year... Launching one tier at 45 pounds, rather than three tiers at once, keeps admin low."
   The problem: The deck shows comparables and picks 45 pounds (the midpoint) but never states why. Is 45 the price that hits the revenue target? Is it the price that maximizes reach? Is it the price the client thinks is right?
   Why it matters: Pricing is a strategic choice, not a default. If Client E needs 6,000 pounds in year-one revenue to justify the program, a lower or higher price might be necessary. The silent pick at midpoint reads as a placeholder, not as strategy the client could defend to the board.
   Do this: State the revenue goal and show that 45 pounds hits it at 8% uptake. If 45 is the right price for other reasons (brand positioning, competitor parity, local income levels), say so. If the team is uncertain between the range, propose testing during soft launch and recommend a final price in month two based on what you learn about the market.
   Answer first: What is the minimum year-one revenue Client E needs membership to earn to count as meaningful diversification?

4. Member benefits are promised without verifying capacity [Moderate]
   Slide 7 and 9 - "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners. These use capacity Client E already has... We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
   The problem: The deck lists benefits and assumes they do not strain the coordinator. It does not verify whether Client E can actually deliver two members-only events a year with one part-time role. Is the coordinator already at capacity? Would members-only events require new volunteer coordination? Is the local partner discount already negotiated or would the coordinator have to build those relationships?
   Why it matters: If the soft launch succeeds and 110 members join (8% of 1,400), the coordinator commits to 220 member-event attendee-days per year plus benefit administration. At part-time hours, this could be infeasible. The risk section flags this as an assumption to test, which is honest. But the team should enter the soft launch knowing what capacity actually exists. Without that, month two becomes a test of whether the assumption was right rather than a test of whether the strategy works.
   Do this: Map what "two members-only events a year" and "local partner discount" require of the coordinator in hours. If both use existing capacity (events Client E already runs, discounts already negotiated), state that. If they require new work, estimate hours and verify with the coordinator that they fit the plan.
   Answer first: Is "two members-only events a year" something Client E already does, or would it be new work the coordinator takes on?

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE
- Whether 8% annual membership conversion is achievable in this client's geography and community without research into comparable case studies. The soft launch will provide this data.
- Whether the local partner discount is already arranged or would require new negotiation. This depends on Client E's existing relationships.
- Whether the board will see a single-tier model as sufficiently ambitious or appropriately cautious. That is a governance decision.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM

3. In the soft launch phase, what specific question is the team trying to answer about 8% conversion, and at what threshold would they recommend stopping instead of going to full launch in month three?
