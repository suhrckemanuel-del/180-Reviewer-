1. Nobody can tell if 8% or 5% makes the scheme viable [Moderate]  
   Slide 8 - "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."  
   Slide 10 - "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target."  
   Slide 10 - "Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."  
   The problem: The deck uses 8% for its income case and 5% for its scale decision without showing why either threshold makes the membership scheme viable.  
   Why it matters: The board is asked to endorse a scheme on a £5,000 case at 8%, while the full-launch decision uses 5%. Without a link between conversion, net income, cost and staff time, the board cannot tell whether 5% proves viability or only a lower result.  
   Do this: Derive both thresholds from one viability test that includes expected income, recurring cost and coordinator time. Show what result supports scaling, redesigning or stopping the scheme.  
   Aim for a line like: If an invented 6% conversion produces enough net income to cover the pilot's measured recurring cost and staff time, Client E can treat the tier as viable to scale.  
   Answer first: What conversion rate produces enough net income to justify the required ongoing work?

2. Existing capacity is assumed, not tested [Moderate]  
   Slide 7 - "These use capacity Client E already has."  
   Slide 9 - "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."  
   Slide 9 - "A soft launch to the most engaged 200 contacts tests both before the full launch."  
   The problem: The plan says the benefits fit existing capacity but provides no workload test that would establish this for a one-person, part-time operation.  
   Why it matters: The board could approve an offer that creates ongoing booking, event and partner-admin work that displaces current activity. With no budget for new staff, the difference between a manageable pilot and an overloaded coordinator changes whether this offer should scale.  
   Do this: Map recurring tasks, hours and handoffs for the pilot and for full launch. Use measured pilot workload as a launch gate, then separate essential benefits from those that can wait.  
   Aim for a line like: If an invented four-hour weekly pilot workload fits within the coordinator's agreed capacity, test the full offer. If it exceeds that capacity, simplify it before scaling.  
   Answer first: How many recurring coordinator hours does each membership benefit require at pilot and full-launch scale?
