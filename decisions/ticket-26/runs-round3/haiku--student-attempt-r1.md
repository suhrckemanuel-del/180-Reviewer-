ON YOUR OWN READ

The market research is well-structured. What your read didn't identify: the comparable pricing data also needs specific attribution to be verifiable by the board.

WHAT WORKS

The soft launch to 200 engaged contacts first is the right gate. The risk section is direct. "The main risk is low conversion. We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff." Assumptions named instead of hidden is rare.

WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 2 high impact, 1 moderate

1. The 8% conversion assumption is called conservative but has no stated reasoning [High impact]
   Slide 8 - "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   The problem: You've tied the 8% conversion rate to the 32% event attendance rate but never explained why attendees who come to 32% of events would pay for membership at 8%. These are different behaviors with different barriers.
   Why it matters: The board's approval depends on trusting this conversion is plausible. A reader might assume you've validated it. If they greenlight the launch expecting 8% and get 4%, they've approved a program that loses money. The board needs to understand what drives the conversion, not just that you've called it conservative.
   Do this: Name what converts an event attendee into a paying member. Is it because engagement with the org justifies the price? Because they value priority booking specifically? Because they know the team well enough to trust the benefits? State this mechanism. Then separately say what evidence would make you confident in this rate.
   Aim for a line like: If <specific driver> converts attendees to members, then <comparable organization or segment> sees <percentage>, which aligns with your 8% target.
   Answer first: What specific behavior or motivation converts an event attendee to a membership buyer?

2. The implementation plan names milestones but not actual first actions [High impact]
   Slide 10 - "Month one: soft launch to 200 contacts."
   The problem: You've said what outcome you want but not what the coordinator will do to make it happen. No action is named: no email template, no message, no outreach method.
   Why it matters: The coordinator cannot start next week without knowing what to actually do. "Soft launch to 200 contacts" is a description of what was supposed to happen, not instructions for how to do it. The team is passing the design work to the executor and leaving the hardest uncertainty unsigned.
   Do this: Walk through the first week. What is the coordinator's first action? Drafting outreach? Building the signup form? Pulling the mailing list? One of these is the real first step and the rest follow. Name it and the next two steps after that.
   Answer first: What does the coordinator do in week one, in a single concrete sentence?

3. The comparable pricing list is sourced to websites but not to specific organizations [Moderate]
   Slide 5 - "Three comparable local arts non-profits run membership at 40 to 60 pounds a year (their public websites, 2025)."
   The problem: You've sourced the data to "public websites" but not named the organizations, so the board has no way to verify the comparables or discuss whether these were the right ones to choose.
   Why it matters: The board might want to ask why you picked these three, or whether Client E should position differently. Without names they cannot. If the board later finds you compared to organizations very different from Client E, the pricing recommendation loses credibility.
   Do this: Name the three organizations specifically so the board can verify or challenge your choice.
   Answer first: Which three organizations did you check, and why those three?

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE
- Whether the 1,400-person mailing list is accurate and well-maintained enough to deliver 8% response
- Whether the "most engaged 200 contacts" are actually identifiable in your database or require manual segmentation
- Whether the benefits (priority booking, members-only events, partner discounts) are feasible without straining the part-time coordinator

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM
1. What evidence from other membership programs or engagement research supports 8% conversion from event attendees, and what specifically about Client E's situation makes you confident in that rate?
2. In week one of the soft launch, what is the single first action the coordinator takes, and what is the one thing you need to know before you can write that email or make that call?
3. How will you identify and segment the "most engaged 200 contacts" — is it already done in your database or would it require manual work?
4. If the soft launch hits 5% (10 conversions from 200), does that generate enough revenue to cover the system costs and coordinator time, or is full launch a go regardless?
5. What is your backup plan if the soft launch hits 5% but the board is still nervous about full launch?
