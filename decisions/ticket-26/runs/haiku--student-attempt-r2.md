WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 1 high impact, 2 moderate, 1 minor

1. The 8% conversion target is unsupported. [High impact]
   Slide 8 - "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   The problem: The 8% is stated as conservative against attendance, but the reasoning is incomplete. Attendance and membership conversion are different behaviors, and no benchmark from the three comparables is offered.
   Why it matters: The board will use this figure to decide whether the scheme is worth launching. If they cannot understand how you arrived at 8%, they cannot evaluate the business case. A rate that is too optimistic masks risk; one too pessimistic masks opportunity. You need to show them either the data or the testing approach.
   Do this: Either source the 8% from comparable-organization interviews or prior similar launches, or reframe the slide to name it as a working hypothesis to be tested in month two. The move is from "this is what we expect" to "this is what we are testing for."
   Answer first: Where did the 8% come from? Is it based on interviews with the comparable organizations about their actual conversion, or internal conversation?

2. The soft launch to 200 contacts is not defined. [Moderate]
   Slide 10 - "Month one: soft launch to 200 contacts."
   The problem: "Soft launch" is summary language but does not say what actions the coordinator takes (email, phone, in-person, direct mail) or how long it will take.
   Why it matters: The coordinator needs to know what Monday morning looks like and what time it will consume. The board needs to understand what soft means and whether the approach is realistic for one part-time coordinator with no new budget. Different outreach methods have vastly different lift. The board cannot evaluate feasibility without knowing this.
   Do this: Name the contact method and sketch the timeline for month one (e.g., email campaign in week one, follow-up in week three, weekly conversion tracking). Say how many hours this will take the coordinator and whether it fits inside existing responsibilities.
   Answer first: What is the most realistic outreach method for this organization and this audience?

3. Member benefits assume capacity without showing it. [Moderate]
   Slide 7 - "These use capacity Client E already has."
   The problem: The slide asserts member benefits will use existing capacity, but the current event calendar and available capacity are never shown.
   Why it matters: Two members-only events a year means about 24 event planning and delivery slots added to the coordinator's workload. The constraint is one part-time coordinator and no new budget. If that capacity does not exist, the value proposition breaks and the recommendation fails. The board cannot commit to benefits you cannot deliver.
   Do this: Show that event capacity exists in the current calendar, or revise member benefits to match available capacity (for example, one member event a year, or a discount-focused benefit that requires no new event production).
   Answer first: How many hours a month does the coordinator currently spend on events and programming, and how much buffer exists?

4. The 1,400 mailing list is stated without a source. [Minor]
   Slide 4 - "Client E has 1,400 people on its mailing list and a 32% event-attendance rate (box-office data, 2024)."
   The problem: The attendance rate is sourced ("box-office data, 2024") but the mailing list is not. It does not need external citation, but it should name the source (email database, newsletter subscribers, prior list, or another system).
   Why it matters: For the board to trust the base population for the conversion calculation, they should know where the 1,400 came from and whether it is current and de-duplicated. If the list includes inactive or duplicate contacts, the true conversion rate will be higher than 8%.
   Do this: Name the source (e.g., "from the organization's email database, most recent pull") and note if de-duplication has been done.
   Answer first: What system is the 1,400 from, and has it been de-duplicated?
