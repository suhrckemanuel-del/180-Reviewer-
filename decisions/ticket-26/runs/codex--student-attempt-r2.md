WHAT WORKS

WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

COUNT: 0 high impact, 3 moderate, 0 minor

1. Nobody can tell whether the conversion targets are credible. [Moderate]  
   Slide 8 - “The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test.”  
   Slide 10 - “Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%.”  
   The problem: The deck treats event attendance as support for an 8% paid-membership conversion assumption, but it does not establish that the two behaviours are comparable or explain the basis for the 5% launch threshold.  
   Why it matters: The board must choose whether to launch and what result should trigger expansion. A tested conversion benchmark supports that choice. An attendance rate measures participation in events, while the proposed thresholds decide whether Client E invests existing coordinator capacity in a wider paid offer.  
   Do this: Set out the evidence or reasoning that connects the target audience to the conversion threshold, then distinguish the pilot success measure from the assumption it is testing. Use a sensitivity range and define what result would change the next step.  
   Answer first: What evidence supports 8% and 5% as appropriate thresholds for paid membership, rather than for event attendance?

2. The case for launch is buried after the board has already passed five topic slides. [Moderate]  
   Slide 1 - “Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn.”  
   Slide 6 - “Launching one tier at 45 pounds, rather than three tiers at once, keeps admin low and lets Client E learn before it scales.”  
   The problem: The first slide states the action but not its reason. The sentence that combines the specific choice with the rationale appears only on Slide 6.  
   Why it matters: The board needs to understand both what it is being asked to approve and why that route fits its capacity before absorbing supporting detail. Without that early connection, the evidence can read as a set of topics instead of a case for the decision.  
   Do this: Bring the decision and its supporting logic together at the start. Make the governing insight show the income need, the limited-capacity design and the test-before-scaling principle in one board-level argument.  
   Answer first: What is the single reason Client E should choose a one-tier pilot now rather than defer membership or launch a broader offer?

3. The staffing risk is acknowledged but not tested against the proposed member offer. [Moderate]  
   Slide 7 - “Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners. These use capacity Client E already has.”  
   Slide 9 - “We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff.”  
   The problem: The deck asserts that the benefits fit existing capacity while separately identifying staff strain as an assumption, without showing what work the part-time coordinator must absorb or what would stop the launch.  
   Why it matters: The board is deciding whether to add a recurring member promise with no budget for new staff. A manageable pilot preserves existing delivery capacity. An untested workload could make Client E scale an offer that crowds out its core work.  
   Do this: Map the recurring tasks created by each benefit, test them in the soft launch and set a workload-based condition for changing or pausing the offer. Keep the member promise proportional to verified capacity.  
   Answer first: Which coordinator tasks are added by booking, events and partner discounts, and what pilot result would show that they cannot be sustained?
