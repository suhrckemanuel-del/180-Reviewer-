WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 0 high impact, 3 moderate, 0 minor

1. The conversion target and expansion gate are not defensible because attendance is used as proof of membership conversion. [Moderate]
   Slide 8 - “The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test.”
   Slide 10 - “Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%.”
   The problem: The deck treats event attendance as a comparator for paid conversion, then uses two unexplained thresholds to govern rollout.
   Why it matters: The board must decide what performance warrants expanding the scheme. Event attendance and paid conversion measure different behaviours. Without relevant conversion evidence or a stated test rationale, the board cannot tell whether a soft-launch result supports expansion or merely clears an arbitrary bar.
   Do this: Establish a named conversion comparator or an internal analogue. Explain the distinct roles of the 8% ambition and 5% expansion threshold.
   Answer first: What comparable paid-offer or membership conversion evidence supports 8%, and why is 5% sufficient for full launch?

2. The capacity risk is named but is not tested before the full-launch decision. [Moderate]
   Slide 9 - “We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff.”
   Slide 10 - “Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%.”
   The problem: Staff capacity is an explicit assumption, while conversion is the only stated condition for proceeding.
   Why it matters: The board needs assurance that a revenue experiment fits existing capacity, not only that contacts might join. The coordinator could meet the conversion gate while bookings, events or partner discounts consume unsustainable time, leading the board to approve an unworkable rollout.
   Do this: Pair the conversion gate with a practical workload measure and a capacity condition for expansion.
   Answer first: What coordinator time and service workload during the soft launch would make full launch feasible on existing capacity?

3. The reason for a single-tier test is buried after the board has already been asked to approve membership. [Moderate]
   Slide 6 - “Launching one tier at 45 pounds, rather than three tiers at once, keeps admin low and lets Client E learn before it scales.”
   The problem: The governing rationale for the proposed scope appears only on Slide 6 rather than in the opening decision statement.
   Why it matters: The board has to approve either a bounded test or a broader membership commitment. The opening asks it to launch before the capacity-and-learning rationale appears. A reader may assess this as a general revenue initiative rather than a controlled experiment, changing the authorization they give.
   Do this: Put one governing insight at the start that joins the single-tier action with its capacity-and-learning rationale. Use later slides as proof.
   Answer first: What precise decision should the board authorize now: a full membership scheme or a bounded test?
