WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

COUNT: 0 high impact, 3 moderate, 0 minor

1. The board never receives one early sentence that states both the launch decision and the evidence-based reason for it. [Moderate]  
   Slide 1 - “Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn.”  
   Slide 2 - “Our answer is yes, with a deliberately small start.”  
   The problem: The action is clear, but its rationale is distributed across later slides rather than stated alongside the recommendation.  
   Why it matters: The board must assemble grant concentration, audience engagement, comparable pricing and capacity constraints itself rather than assess one complete rationale. That makes approval of a £45 membership dependent on evidence fragments instead of a decision they can evaluate in one pass.  
   Do this: Open with one governing insight that joins the recommended action to the few pieces of evidence that justify it.  
   Answer first: Which evidence points most directly explain why a single £45 tier is the right response to income concentration now?

2. The £5,000 case and rollout target rely on an 8% assumption that attendance does not validate as paid conversion. [Moderate]  
   Slide 4 - “Client E has 1,400 people on its mailing list and a 32% event-attendance rate (box-office data, 2024).”  
   Slide 8 - “If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one.”  
   Slide 8 - “The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test.”  
   The problem: The deck calls 8% conservative against event attendance, but provides no paid-membership conversion evidence or range to establish whether that comparison supports the revenue case.  
   Why it matters: The board is choosing whether expected membership income justifies coordinator time and member benefits. A turnout measure and a willingness-to-pay measure can lead to different conclusions, so £5,000 may be interpreted as a supported return when it remains an untested assumption.  
   Do this: Ground the conversion case in a relevant benchmark or pilot result, then show how the recommendation changes across plausible conversion outcomes.  
   Answer first: How many of the 200 soft-launch contacts join at £45, and what paid-conversion range does that result support?

3. The rollout leaves the board without a stated action for a pilot result between 5% and 8% conversion. [Moderate]  
   Slide 10 - “Month two: review conversion against the 8% target.”  
   Slide 10 - “Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%.”  
   The problem: The deck names an 8% target and a 5% launch gate, but does not explain how either threshold governs the decision.  
   Why it matters: At 5% to 7.9%, the board cannot tell whether Client E should launch despite missing the stated target or pause and revise the offer. Those actions have different demands on the part-time coordinator and different implications for expected income.  
   Do this: Define the decision rule for each relevant pilot outcome and connect it to income value, member offer quality and available coordinator capacity.  
   Answer first: At each soft-launch conversion range, what action is Client E authorised to take, and why?
