ON YOUR OWN READ
You read the Monday morning gap correctly: the deliverable explains what to do strategically but not what the coordinator actually does on day one. Where your read missed the higher risk: the 8% conversion assumption is the lynchpin, and it has no benchmark. The pricing source concern is fair too - the comparability of those three organizations is not established, which matters for defending the 45 pound price point.

WHAT WORKS
- The recommendation is concrete and operationally simple: "Launching one tier at 45 pounds, rather than three tiers at once, keeps admin low and lets Client E learn before it scales" (Slide 6). This is smart thinking about constraints.
- The soft launch structure is sound risk management: "soft launch to the most engaged 200 contacts tests both before the full launch" (Slide 9). Testing conversion before full rollout is the right call for an org with no membership history.
- The problem framing rests on real 2024 data: "Project grants are 80% of Client E's income (2024 accounts) and each is single-year. Two of five grants are not expected to renew, per the funder letters on file" (Slide 3). This creates clear urgency.

WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 1 high impact, 3 moderate, 0 minor

1. The 8% conversion assumption drives the financial outcome but carries no benchmark. [High impact]
   Slide 8 - "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one. The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   The problem: The deck calls 8% "conservative" but provides no data from comparable organizations to support it. Attending events and paying for membership are different behaviors, so comparing to the 32% attendance rate does not ground the number. The board has no basis to assess whether 8% is credible.
   Why it matters: Client E's board is approving a strategy whose financial case rests entirely on this single assumption. If the true conversion is 3-4%, the projected 5,000 pounds becomes insufficient to justify staff time and operational change. The board needs either the source of 8% or a clear understanding that this is a hypothesis, not a forecast.
   Do this: Find the actual conversion rate from at least one of the three comparable organizations named in Slide 5, or from another arts non-profit with an established membership scheme, and state what benchmark you are against. If no comparable data exists, separate the assumption (8% is our starting hypothesis) from the outcome (5,000 pounds only if that holds), and emphasize that month two testing is the real answer.
   Aim for a line like: If comparable membership schemes achieve 6-10% conversion from engaged audiences, then 8% is appropriately positioned; if they achieve 2-3%, then the assumption is optimistic and month two is critical. That is the shape, not your numbers.
   Answer first: What conversion rate do comparable arts organizations achieve with their membership launches?

2. The comparability of the three pricing benchmarks is not established. [Moderate]
   Slide 5 - "Three comparable local arts non-profits run membership at 40 to 60 pounds a year (their public websites, 2025). Client E has no membership today."
   The problem: The deck cites three organizations' pricing but does not explain why they are comparable to Client E. Arts non-profits vary in scale, audience, and pricing power. A large established ensemble will price differently than a grassroots community group. The board does not know whether 40-60 pounds is appropriate for Client E specifically.
   Why it matters: If the comparable organizations are larger or serve wealthier audiences than Client E, their pricing may not apply. Client E could be over-pricing and suppressing early conversion, or under-pricing and leaving income on the table.
   Do this: Name the three organizations and state their annual attendance or operating budget. Explicitly note where they are similar to Client E (audience profile, size, scope) and where they differ. If they differ materially in scale, adjust the price recommendation or note that the soft launch will test Client E's actual price elasticity.
   Aim for a line like: If the three organizations each see 4,000+ annual attendees and Client E sees roughly 450 (32% of 1,400 on the list), then they operate at a different scale; clarify whether their pricing is relevant. That is the shape, not your numbers.
   Answer first: What are the annual attendance figures or operating budgets of the three comparable organizations?

3. The soft launch execution is missing the Monday morning action. [Moderate]
   Slide 10 - "Month one: soft launch to 200 contacts."
   The problem: The deliverable says the coordinator will "soft launch to 200 contacts" but does not specify how. Are the 200 drawn from event attendance records, from volunteer lists, or from overall mailing list engagement? Will outreach be by email, phone call, or personal invite? What does the message say? The coordinator cannot start on Monday without these details.
   Why it matters: The client has real constraints: "One part-time coordinator. No budget for new staff." Every execution choice has different time costs and likely different conversion rates. Personal outreach converts differently than email. The coordinator needs to know which approach to use, or which to decide collaboratively with the team, before the month starts.
   Do this: For month one, name one specific action the coordinator takes. Example: "Email the 200 most engaged mailing list members (those who attended 3+ events in 2024) with a landing page and an invitation to a members-only preview event on [date]." You find the actual approach, but it needs that level of specificity.
   Aim for a line like: If the outreach is a personalized email to the 200 highest-engagement contacts with a launch-exclusive early-bird discount, then the coordinator can execute it in the time available. That is the shape, not your numbers.
   Answer first: How are the 200 most engaged contacts identified, and what is the outreach mechanism?

4. The 5,000 pound projection lacks context to Client E's overall budget. [Moderate]
   Slide 8 - "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one."
   The problem: The projection is stated without saying whether 5,000 pounds is material to Client E's finances. If Client E operates on a 120,000 pound budget, this is 4%. If it is on a 300,000 pound budget, it is less than 2%. The board cannot assess the strategic priority of this work without knowing the scale.
   Why it matters: If 5,000 pounds represents 10-15% of grant income, launching a membership scheme is transformational work and merits significant effort. If it is 2-3%, it is an enhancement. The board's decision on sequencing and resource allocation depends on this.
   Do this: State Client E's total annual operating budget or grant income somewhere visible (Slide 3 would fit naturally). Then in Slide 8, say what percentage of current income the 5,000 pounds represents.
   Aim for a line like: If Client E operates on a 100,000 pound annual budget and grants account for 80,000 pounds, then 5,000 pounds in year one is 5% of total operating income, which is material enough to justify the launch effort. That is the shape, not your numbers.
   Answer first: What is Client E's annual operating budget or total annual income from all sources?

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE
- Whether the three named organizations are genuinely comparable to Client E in scale, audience type, or operating model.
- Whether the members-only events can sustain the staffing assumption (the deliverable assumes they fit in existing capacity, which the soft launch will test, but the event design detail that would confirm this is not visible here).
- Whether a month-two review of 200 soft launches has sufficient statistical power to distinguish 8% conversion from 5% conversion reliably.
- Whether 45 pounds is the right price point given Client E's specific cost of service and audience willingness to pay.
