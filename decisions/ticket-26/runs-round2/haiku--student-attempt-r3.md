WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 1 high impact, 3 moderate, 0 minor

1. The soft launch is described in timeline, not mechanism. [High impact]
   Slide 10 - "Month one: soft launch to 200 contacts."
   The problem: You cannot execute "soft launch" without knowing the channel. The slide names the when (month one) and the target (200 contacts) but not the how. Will the coordinator send an email? Make calls? Hand-write letters? The board does not know if the launch is feasible because it does not know what the coordinator is doing.
   Why it matters: The coordinator will ask for clarification before acting. If the team has to explain in a meeting what "soft launch" means, the timeline slips. If the team was unclear about effort, the plan falls apart on contact with reality.
   Do this: Describe the soft launch as one concrete action. Name the channel (email, phone, post, or one combination). If it is email, state one sample line from the message so the coordinator sees the tone. Do not write the full email or the full script, but make clear what action the coordinator takes in month one.
   Aim for a line like: If the team chooses email outreach, then "the coordinator sends a brief message to 200 members naming the benefit (priority booking) and the tier price, and collects responses over one month."
   Answer first: Is the soft launch email, phone calls, in-person, or a mix of these?

2. The drop from 32% attendance to 8% conversion is not explained. [Moderate]
   Slide 8 - "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   The problem: The board sees 32% of 1,400 people already engage with events. The slide does not explain why paying members would be much smaller. This is the headline number the recommendation rests on, and the reasoning is missing.
   Why it matters: The board cannot evaluate whether 8% is plausible if you do not show the math. They might approve something that has no chance because you did not anchor the assumption to comparable data or prior experience.
   Do this: Explain one reason for the drop. What do comparable non-profits see when they price membership at 45 pounds to attendees who have been free? Is the 8% figure from those non-profits, or is it conservative against industry benchmarks? Is it discounted for the friction of asking attendees to pay? Use the research you did on comparable pricing to justify the conversion rate.
   Aim for a line like: If <the three comparable non-profits you researched saw average conversion of 10-15% from free attendees to paid members>, then "8% is conservative against that benchmark."
   Answer first: What is the typical conversion rate from free attendee to paid member at comparable arts non-profits?

3. The members-only benefits are too vague to execute. [Moderate]
   Slide 7 - "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners."
   The problem: The coordinator will not know what "priority booking" means in operation. Is it a waitlist? A 48-hour head start? The same vagueness applies to "discount with local partners"—which partners, and how much discount? The team describes what members get; the client cannot actually deliver it.
   Why it matters: If the plan launches with these benefits vague, members will be confused about what they bought. The coordinator will improvise, leading to inconsistency. You cannot build a value prop on "we'll figure it out during month one."
   Do this: For each benefit, describe one concrete form of execution in one sentence. You do not have to finalize every detail, but make it clear how a member experiences the benefit. For partners, name one if you have lined them up, or state what you will do (e.g., "identify and approach five local partners for 10% discounts").
   Aim for a line like: For priority booking, "members receive an email 48 hours before the general public with a direct purchase link for priority-sold-out events."
   Answer first: Which specific local partners have agreed to offer members a discount, and what is the discount amount and code?

4. The revenue adequacy is never stated. [Moderate]
   Slides 8-10, across all three months - The entire case rests on "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one." The slide then sets a go/no-go rule (soft launch clears 5% conversion) but never states a revenue target.
   The problem: The board does not know whether 5,000 pounds justifies the launch. If conversion hits 12%, is that success or a sign the coordinator is overwhelmed? If it hits 4%, is that failure or iteration? Month two review needs a decision criterion tied to revenue, not just conversion percentage.
   Why it matters: The team might approve full launch based on hitting 8%, only to discover 5,000 pounds was never enough to sustain the program. Or the opposite: the client is thrilled at 5,000 pounds and the team overshoots without a plan for scaling. The decision rule is incomplete.
   Do this: State the revenue figure that matters. What annual membership revenue justifies hiring someone part-time to own this, or justifies the coordinator's time as redeployed from other work? Tie the month-two go/no-go decision to that number, not to a conversion percentage alone.
   Aim for a line like: If <the team's cost model shows X pounds annually makes this sustainable>, then "month two review will approve full launch only if the soft launch is tracking to X pounds in year one."
   Answer first: What is the minimum annual revenue from membership that makes this programme sustainable for Client E?

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE
Whether the price point of 45 pounds is optimised for this segment. You have good research on comparable non-profits, but optimisation for this specific client requires feedback you do not have.
Whether the members-only events are truly feasible within the one coordinator's capacity once the programme is real. The risks name this assumption; execution detail is needed to verify it.
Whether the identifiable "local partners" exist and would take a discount offer. This requires real conversations.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM
1. How will the 200 "most engaged" contacts be identified: from email open rates, prior attendance, ticket purchase history, or some combination?
2. Do you have data from any of the three comparable non-profits you researched on what percentage of their free-tier or attendees convert to paid members?
3. If the soft launch hits 6% conversion (above the 5% go threshold but below the 8% assumption), what does the team recommend: iterate and relaunch, or proceed to full launch and plan to adjust?
4. Are the two members-only events scheduled to launch with the programme, or added later? This affects how members perceive value at signup.
5. Will the coordinator track non-response after the soft launch, and if so, how will feedback inform the full launch messaging?
