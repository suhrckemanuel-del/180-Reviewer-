WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

COUNT: 1 high impact, 2 moderate, 0 minor

1. The conversion case does not yet support the full-launch decision because it calls 8% conservative against attendance, then expands at a different 5% threshold. [High impact]  
   Where: Slides 8 and 10  
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."  
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."  
   Why it costs the client: The board may approve a scheme expecting the result implied by 8%, while the operational plan treats a materially lower result as sufficient to expand. The difference determines whether the income gain justifies the coordinator's workload.  
   What to do: Separate the forecast assumption from the scale-up gate. Ground the conversion assumption in a relevant membership comparator or a transparent test design, then state what conversion result makes the scheme worthwhile given expected workload.  
   Answer first: What evidence supports 8% conversion, and what minimum conversion rate makes a full launch worthwhile?

2. The governing insight is absent from the opening because the first two slides state the action but not the reason that makes it the board's preferred choice. [Moderate]  
   Where: Slides 1 and 2  
   Quote: "Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn."  
   Quote: "Client E asked whether a paid membership could diversify income away from project grants. Our answer is yes, with a deliberately small start."  
   Why it costs the client: The board must assemble the rationale from later slides rather than receiving the action and its reason together. That makes approval depend on connecting grant exposure, demand evidence and the launch test during the read.  
   What to do: Put one governing insight first that links the single-tier launch to the grant-renewal exposure and the evidence that makes a limited test preferable to either inaction or a larger rollout.  
   Answer first: What is the one reason Client E should choose a limited membership launch now rather than retain the current income mix?

3. The capacity risk is acknowledged but the soft launch has no measurable test for whether the part-time coordinator can sustain membership delivery. [Moderate]  
   Where: Slides 6, 9 and 10  
   Quote: "Launching one tier at 45 pounds, rather than three tiers at once, keeps admin low and lets Client E learn before it scales."  
   Quote: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."  
   Quote: "The Client E coordinator owns the launch."  
   Why it costs the client: The board must weigh new income against staff capacity, but the plan only tests conversion. A launch can meet its conversion threshold while still creating work the coordinator cannot absorb without weakening existing activity.  
   What to do: Define the operational measures, capacity limit and response that the soft launch will test alongside conversion. Use those measures to make rollout conditional on both demand and deliverability.  
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours and event-delivery tasks will the pilot require, and what level would make full launch impractical?

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM

- What is the source or calculation behind the 8% conversion assumption, beyond the 32% attendance rate?
- What annual income or conversion threshold makes the coordinator workload worthwhile?
- Why is 5% sufficient for full launch if 8% is the stated target?
- How will the team define and select the 200 "most engaged" contacts?
- What staff-time and service-quality measures will determine whether the pilot is operationally sustainable?
