WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 0 high impact, 3 moderate, 0 minor

1. The opening recommendation does not state in one sentence both why paid membership should launch and why a single-tier autumn start is the choice. [Moderate]
   Where: Slides 1 and 2
   Quote: "Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn."
   Quote: "Our answer is yes, with a deliberately small start."
   Why it costs the client: A board member can see the proposed action but must join grant exposure, audience engagement and risk reduction across later slides before judging it. That makes an approval decision less defensible than one based on an explicit decision case.
   What to do: Open with one governing insight that joins the launch decision to its decisive evidence-based reason. Use later slides as proof.
   Answer first: What single reason should make the board choose a single-tier autumn launch now rather than wait?

2. The conversion forecast and full-launch gate lack a membership-relevant comparator or an economic threshold. [Moderate]
   Where: Slides 8 and 10
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Quote: "Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The board could approve a full launch at 5% even if it produces too little income for the coordinator time required, or reject a viable launch because 8% is missed. The deck does not connect either rate to required value or comparable conversion.
   What to do: Test the 8% assumption against membership-like evidence and set the full-launch threshold from incremental income, direct costs and coordinator capacity. Show what the comparison implies.
   Answer first: What conversion rate produces enough net value to justify ongoing coordinator time, and what concrete comparator supports expecting it?

3. The benefit package is presented as fitting existing capacity while its staff impact remains an assumption. [Moderate]
   Where: Slides 7 and 9
   Quote: "These use capacity Client E already has."
   Quote: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
   Why it costs the client: The board could approve benefits the part-time coordinator cannot sustain, rather than authorising a lower-touch pilot or deferring expansion until workload fit is known. A soft launch tests demand but does not itself quantify the continuing operating burden.
   What to do: Convert the capacity assertion into a workload test covering launch, member support, events and partner discounts. Define the fallback if the workload exceeds available time.
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours per month will each membership activity require after launch?
