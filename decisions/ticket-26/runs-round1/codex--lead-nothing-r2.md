WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 1 high impact, 2 moderate, 1 minor

1. The board has no defensible conversion rate or economic threshold for approving the scheme. [High impact]
   Where: Slides 8 and 10
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The board is choosing between a full rollout and preserving scarce capacity for existing work. Event attendance is not a membership-conversion benchmark, while the 5% gate is not connected to costs or minimum useful income. The board could approve a scheme that falls short of its financial purpose.
   What to do: Calculate the scheme's contribution after administration, events, discounts and partner costs. Derive the break-even conversion rate, explain why 5% is the rollout gate and present 8% as an untested scenario unless a relevant benchmark supports it.
   Answer first: What is the lowest conversion rate at which membership generates enough net income to justify the coordinator's time?
   Aim for a line like: At £900 annual delivery cost, the scheme breaks even at 1.4% conversion: that is the shape, not your numbers.

2. Testing only the most engaged contacts cannot validate conversion across the full mailing list. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 9
   Quote: "A soft launch to the most engaged 200 contacts tests both before the full launch."
   Why it costs the client: The board must choose between scaling and gathering more evidence. The warmest 200 contacts are likely to respond differently from the remaining 1,200, so a successful pilot could overstate full-list revenue and trigger premature rollout.
   What to do: Define the pilot as a test of warm-cohort demand and operational feasibility. Either test a representative sample or forecast the remaining cohort separately before authorising full rollout.
   Answer first: What conversion result among the remaining 1,200 contacts would make rollout worthwhile?
   Aim for a line like: The warm cohort converted at 10%, while the remaining list must convert at 4% to cover annual delivery cost: that is the shape, not your numbers.

3. The rollout gate ignores the staff-strain risk the pilot is supposed to test. [Moderate]
   Where: Slides 9 and 10
   Quote: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The coordinator could exceed the conversion threshold while the benefits prove unmanageable. A conversion-only gate would tell the board to scale even when the pilot shows that existing capacity cannot support the offer.
   What to do: Add measurable operational criteria covering administration time, event delivery and partner management. Make those criteria part of the scale, revise or stop decision.
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours per month can membership consume without displacing current work?
   Aim for a line like: Proceed only if conversion exceeds 5% and administration remains below six coordinator hours a month: that is the shape, not your numbers.

4. No single sentence gives the recommendation and its reason together. [Minor]
   Where: Slide 1
   Quote: "Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn."
   Why it costs the client: The board receives the action immediately but must assemble its rationale from later slides. The recommendation still lands, yet the central case is less likely to survive a fast read or be repeated accurately in discussion.
   What to do: Put the recommended action and its strongest decision reason into one early governing sentence. Keep the detailed evidence on the later slides.
   Answer first: What is the single strongest reason the board should choose membership now?
