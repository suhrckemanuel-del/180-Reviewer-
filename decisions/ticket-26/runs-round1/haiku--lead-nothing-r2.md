WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 1 high impact, 2 moderate

1. The soft launch success threshold does not logically support the full launch target [High impact]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: If the most engaged 200 contacts convert at 5%, the board will expect the full mailing list to convert at less, not 8%. The board needs to understand why proceeding from 5% soft launch conversion to 8% full launch conversion is justified.
   What to do: Explain the logic explicitly. If the soft launch is a go/no-go gate (5% means the program is viable regardless of scaling), say so. If you expect engagement to drop but still reach 8%, name the factors that bridge the gap. If you expect the full launch to fall short of 8%, revise your target and the financial model.
   Answer first: If the most engaged 200 members convert at 5%, what conversion rate should you expect from the full 1,400 mailing list?

2. The 8% conversion assumption lacks an external reference point [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The board must approve membership infrastructure investment based on 8% conversion. The 32% attendance rate is not a comparable metric. Without external benchmark data, the board cannot judge whether 8% is realistic, optimistic, or pessimistic.
   What to do: Research what conversion rate comparable non-profits achieve from their membership campaigns. Add one data point: "Similar organizations achieve X% conversion, so our 8% assumption is..." If comparable data is unavailable, note that the soft launch serves as the reality check.
   Answer first: What membership conversion rate do comparable local arts non-profits achieve?

3. Mailing list accuracy is named as a risk but not verified [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 9
   Quote: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
   Why it costs the client: The financial model depends on 1,400 active mailing list members. Without recent validation, the list may contain inactive addresses, shrinking the effective audience. The client needs to know whether the list is clean or requires cleaning before launch.
   What to do: Before soft launch, verify the mailing list. Pull recent email campaign data - what bounce rate have the last three campaigns shown? If the list is clean, say so explicitly. If it needs cleaning, build that into month one before soft launch begins.
   Answer first: When was the mailing list last validated, and what bounce rate do recent campaigns show?

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE
- Whether 45 pounds is optimal pricing for Client E's specific market and audience
- Whether the members-only benefits will actually drive conversion at the assumed rate
- Whether Client E's operations can deliver the promised member experience at scale without straining core programs

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM
1. If 5% soft launch conversion is the gate to proceed, what conversion rate are you actually expecting from the full mailing list, and what is the logic connecting the two?
2. Have you researched what membership conversion rates comparable local arts non-profits achieve?
3. When was the mailing list last cleaned, and what bounce rate does it show in recent campaigns?
4. Will the soft launch include A/B testing on messaging or pricing to inform the full launch strategy?
5. How will you measure whether members-only events strain staff capacity, and what would trigger a scaling pause?
