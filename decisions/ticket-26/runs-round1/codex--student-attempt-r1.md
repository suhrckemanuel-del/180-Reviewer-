WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 0 high impact, 4 moderate, 0 minor

1. The deck gives the board two success thresholds without saying which one controls the launch. [Moderate]
   Slide 10 - "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   The problem: A result between 5% and 8% is simultaneously below the stated target and above the full-launch threshold, with no explanation of the distinction.
   Why it matters: The board could treat the same pilot result as a miss requiring another test or as a pass triggering full launch. Those choices commit different amounts of the coordinator's limited capacity.
   Do this: Define one go or no-go threshold and distinguish it from any stretch target. Base the threshold on acceptable net income and workload, not conversion alone.
   Answer first: What exactly should Client E do if the pilot converts 6% of contacts?

2. The reason for the recommendation appears too late for a board reading once. [Moderate]
   Slide 6 - "Launching one tier at 45 pounds, rather than three tiers at once, keeps admin low and lets Client E learn before it scales."
   The problem: The sentence that joins the recommended action to its risk-management rationale does not appear until slide 6.
   Why it matters: The board first encounters a general membership proposal, then only later learns that the real choice is a bounded test designed around limited capacity. That distinction can change whether it authorises the pilot.
   Do this: Put the governing insight at the start, with the choice and its reason in one sentence. Let subsequent slides prove that statement.
   Answer first: What single reason makes this specific launch design preferable for Client E?

3. The 8% case does not show whether membership is realistic or financially meaningful. [Moderate]
   Slide 8 - "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one."
   Slide 8 - "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   The problem: Event attendance is not a relevant benchmark for paid conversion, while 5,000 pounds is not compared with the expected grant loss, an income target or delivery costs.
   Why it matters: The board cannot tell whether the scheme would replace a meaningful share of vulnerable income or consume scarce capacity for a small net return. A relevant comparison could support a launch, a smaller test or no launch.
   Do this: Support 8% with a relevant paid-membership benchmark or show a scenario range. Compare net membership income with the non-renewing grants and the minimum diversification need.
   Aim for a line like: If the non-renewing grants total 12,000 pounds, 5,000 pounds replaces 42% before delivery costs; that is the shape, not your numbers.
   Answer first: What minimum net income would make the scheme worthwhile for Client E?

4. The soft launch cannot by itself prove that the full offer fits existing staff capacity. [Moderate]
   Slide 9 - "A soft launch to the most engaged 200 contacts tests both before the full launch."
   The problem: A small outreach test can reveal initial administration problems, but it does not establish the workload of serving the full list and running two members-only events.
   Why it matters: The board may approve benefits that force the coordinator to choose between membership delivery and core operations. Evidence of higher workload could require a narrower offer, a smaller rollout or a stop decision.
   Do this: Estimate recurring administration and event hours at pilot and full-launch volumes. Set a capacity limit alongside the conversion threshold.
   Aim for a line like: At 112 members, 15 minutes of monthly administration per member adds 28 staff hours a month before events; that is the shape, not your numbers.
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours per month can the scheme use without displacing core work?
