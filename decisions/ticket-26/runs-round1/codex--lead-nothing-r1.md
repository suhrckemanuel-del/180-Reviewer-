WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 1 high impact, 3 moderate, 0 minor

1. The deck tells the board to use two different launch thresholds. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target."
   Quote: "Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: At 6% conversion, one instruction treats the pilot as below target while the other triggers a full launch. The board cannot tell whether Client E should launch, adjust the offer or run a longer test.
   What to do: Set one decision rule and define the action for results below, near and above it. Link that threshold to the minimum outcome that makes expansion worthwhile.
   Answer first: What should Client E do if conversion is between 5% and 7.9%?

2. The 8% conversion claim is not yet defensible. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: Event attendance and willingness to pay £45 are different behaviours. Without evidence connecting them, the board cannot treat 8% as conservative or rely on the £5,000 scenario when setting the pilot target and deciding whether to expand.
   What to do: Present 8% as a hypothesis rather than a supported benchmark. Add a scenario range and identify evidence that would connect engagement to paid conversion.
   Answer first: What evidence shows how many event attendees or engaged contacts would pay £45?

3. The benefits promise rests on untested staff capacity. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 7
   Quote: "These use capacity Client E already has."
   Where: Slide 9
   Quote: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
   Why it costs the client: The board could approve two additional events and partner administration that the part-time coordinator cannot absorb. That could force Client E to reduce the offer, delay delivery or spend money it does not have.
   What to do: Estimate the coordinator hours and direct costs required for recruitment, member support, events and partner management. Test a minimum viable benefit package against the available capacity.
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours per month can membership use without displacing existing work?

4. The opening never gives the board the recommendation and its reason together. [Moderate]
   Where: Slide 1
   Quote: "Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn."
   Where: Slide 2
   Quote: "Our answer is yes, with a deliberately small start."
   Why it costs the client: A fast reader gets the decision but not why this specific design is appropriate. The board may retain “launch membership” while missing that the recommendation depends on a low-admin offer and a staged test of uncertain demand.
   What to do: Make the opening governing insight combine the recommended action with the reason for the single-tier pilot. Then ensure the following slide titles prove that statement in sequence.
   Answer first: What single reason makes a staged one-tier launch preferable to either no launch or an immediate full launch?

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE
Whether the three organisations are sufficiently comparable to Client E.
Whether the mailing list is current, permissioned and active.
Whether £45 is acceptable to Client E's target audience.
Whether the proposed partner discounts have been agreed.
Whether the coordinator has enough spare capacity to operate the scheme.
Whether £5,000 is gross income or a meaningful net contribution after delivery costs.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM
1. What financial or strategic outcome would make the pilot worth expanding?
2. What should happen if conversion falls between 5% and 7.9%?
3. What conversion evidence exists beyond the event-attendance rate?
4. What are the expected annual costs and coordinator hours for the proposed benefits?
5. Can the soft launch test staff strain before the members-only events are delivered?
