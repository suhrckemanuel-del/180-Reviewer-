WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 1 high impact, 2 moderate, 0 minor

1. The deck gives the board two different launch thresholds. [High impact]
   Slide 10 - "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target."
   Slide 10 - "Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   The problem: You ask the coordinator to review against 8% but authorize full launch above 5%, without saying whether 5% is a floor and 8% is a target.
   Why it matters: A 6% result leads to two different actions. If 8% is the gate, the coordinator pauses. If 5% is the gate, the coordinator expands, committing scarce capacity under a result the deck also treats as below target.
   Do this: Define one go or no-go rule or label the floor and target separately. State what happens below 5%, between 5% and 8% and at or above 8%.
   Answer first: Is 5% the minimum launch gate and 8% the year-one target, or is 8% the launch gate?

2. The board cannot tell whether 8% or £5,000 is enough. [Moderate]
   Slide 8 - "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one. The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Slide 3 - "Project grants are 80% of Client E's income (2024 accounts) and each is single-year."
   The problem: You compare paid conversion with attendance, not paid behavior, and never compare £5,000 with the at-risk grant value, delivery cost or a diversification target.
   Why it matters: The board is choosing between a scheme that creates meaningful resilience and one that adds work for little protection. Attendance does not separate those outcomes, while £5,000 gross may be material or immaterial depending on the funding gap and delivery cost.
   Do this: Replace the attendance comparison with a paid-conversion benchmark or a transparent scenario range. Compare net proceeds with the non-renewing grant value and the minimum worthwhile contribution.
   Aim for a line like: If the two non-renewing grants total £40,000 and delivery costs £1,200, £5,000 gross closes about 10% of the gap: that is the shape, not your numbers.
   Answer first: What minimum net income makes membership worth the coordinator's time, and what paid-conversion evidence supports the forecast range?

3. The soft launch cannot test the two risks it claims to test. [Moderate]
   Slide 9 - "A soft launch to the most engaged 200 contacts tests both before the full launch."
   The problem: Selecting the most engaged contacts biases conversion upward, while operating at low volume does not establish whether the benefits and administration fit existing capacity at full scale.
   Why it matters: The board may scale from a warm cohort that converts better than the wider list, while the coordinator inherits a workload the pilot never reproduced. An encouraging trial could therefore lead to an underperforming launch or exceed existing capacity.
   Do this: Separate the demand and capacity tests. Compare segments, record administrative time and model the workload at full scale before making the launch decision.
   Aim for a line like: If each signup takes 15 minutes and 5% of 1,400 contacts join, signup administration alone requires 17.5 hours: that is the shape, not your numbers.
   Answer first: What evidence from 200 contacts would justify conclusions about conversion and workload across all 1,400?
