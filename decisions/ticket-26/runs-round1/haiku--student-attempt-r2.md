WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 1 high impact, 3 moderate, 1 minor

1. The 8% conversion assumption drives the financial case but has no empirical basis [High impact]
   Slide 8 - "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   The problem: This is the only figure the financial plan rests on. You state the number (8%) and make a claim (it is conservative) but provide no evidence for either. No benchmark. No comparable. No research showing why 8% is defensible or where it came from.
   Why it matters: The board is being asked to approve launch based on £5,000 year-one revenue, entirely dependent on 8% conversion. If actual conversion is 3%, revenue is £1,875 instead. The client must know what the number rests on before authorizing spend.
   Do this: Show your reasoning behind 8%. Is it based on comparable non-profits' conversion? Sector benchmarks? Peer feedback? If you lack that data, name the gap explicitly and say how you would close it before full launch.
   Aim for a line like: "Comparable arts non-profits we studied converted 8-12% of event attendees to membership in year one."
   Answer first: What is the conversion rate from event attendee to paid member in non-profits similar to Client E?

2. The link between 32% attendance and 8% membership conversion is stated but not explained [Moderate]
   Slide 8 - "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate."
   The problem: You claim 8% is conservative given 32% attendance, but you do not show the reasoning connecting them. Is 8% conservative because it is one-quarter of attendance? Because sector norms say so? Because you tested it with a sample?
   Why it matters: A reader cannot evaluate whether the assumption is sound without seeing how you derived it. You are asking the board to act on a relationship between two numbers that you have not explained.
   Do this: Spell out the path from 32% attendance to 8% membership. Name the rule or source behind each step. Show how you know 8% is conservative and not aggressive.
   Answer first: What percentage of people who attend events at similar non-profits become paid members?

3. Two members-only events per year assumes coordinator capacity you have not tested [Moderate]
   Slide 7 - "Two members-only events a year" | Slide 9 - "we assume...that members-only events do not strain staff."
   The problem: You promise members two exclusive events annually, but Slide 9 lists this as an untested assumption. The brief says one part-time coordinator, no new budget. You cannot know the promise is deliverable until you try. If soft launch reveals the coordinator is overloaded, the membership value proposition is weaker than you advertised.
   Why it matters: Members renew because of what they get, not because of price. If you cannot deliver two events per year, differentiation erodes and churn rises.
   Do this: Build a capacity test into the soft launch. Define what "straining staff" means in hours. If soft launch breaches that threshold, revise the offer before scaling to full launch.
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours does one members-only event require, and can Client E realistically sustain two per year without hiring?

4. "Discount with local partners" is committed without confirming partner agreement [Moderate]
   Slide 7 - "a discount with local partners"
   The problem: You list this as part of the membership offer but do not mention confirming it with any partners. This is a third-party commitment you cannot make alone.
   Why it matters: If partners decline or offer token discounts, members feel misled. The membership is less attractive and renewal rates fall.
   Do this: Before soft launch, confirm which local partners will offer a discount and at what depth. Document it. If no partners have been approached, that is the first step before any launch.
   Answer first: Which local partners has Client E already asked, and what discount depth is realistic?

5. The gate to scale from soft launch to full launch (5% conversion) has no explicit reasoning [Minor]
   Slide 10 - "Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   The problem: The plan assumes 8% year-one conversion but uses 5% as the threshold to scale. You do not explain why. Is 5% a success trigger or just a data-gathering milestone?
   Why it matters: It is unclear what "pass" means. If 5% is good enough, why plan the full year on 8%? If 8% is the real target, what happens if soft launch hits 6%?
   Do this: Decide whether 5% is a go-or-no-go gate or a stepping stone toward 8%. State your reasoning for that specific threshold.
   Answer first: If soft launch converts 5% of the 200 most engaged contacts, will full launch be financially viable and operationally sound?
