WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER
COUNT: 1 high impact, 1 minor

1. The 8% conversion assumption that drives the financial case is stated without showing the methodology or benchmark behind it. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The board will ask how the team arrived at 8%. Without a clear answer the case rests on soft-launch results rather than evidence, shifting the pitch from "research shows this works" to "we hope this works." This weakens the recommendation's defensibility before the board approves the commitment.
   What to do: State the methodology plainly. Is 8% a percentage of event attendees (e.g., one in four attendees converting to members)? Is it based on external research on arts membership conversion? Is it informed judgment, and if so, what principle supports this number over 10% or 5%? Make the reasoning transparent so the board understands the choice.
   Answer first: What specific methodology or reference point supports 8%, not some other number?

2. Whether the part-time coordinator can absorb two members-only events annually is listed as an assumption to test but has not been verified with the coordinator before board presentation. [Minor]
   Where: Slides 7 and 9
   Quote: Slide 7: "These use capacity Client E already has." / Slide 9: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
   Why it costs the client: Verifying feasibility beforehand ensures the recommendation is implementable as stated. If the coordinator cannot manage two events within current hours, the benefits change and the recommendation changes with it. A conversation before the board is safer than discovering it after approval.
   What to do: Confirm with the coordinator that two members-only events per year fit within their existing workload without additional support. If not, scope what support would be needed and decide whether to revise the benefit offer or the recommendation path accordingly.
   Answer first: Has the coordinator confirmed they can support two events annually, and are there constraints you should know about?

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE

- Whether the named benefits (priority booking, members-only events, local partner discount) are features the audience actually values
- Whether the existing discount partnerships are already established or speculative

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM
