ON YOUR OWN READ

You were right to focus on the 8% assumption and on whether the recommendation becomes usable. You missed that the deck does give an immediate owner and sequence in Slide 10, but it does not connect that sequence to a clear decision rule or prove the income case it is meant to test.

WHAT WORKS

- Slide 3 identifies a concrete income exposure: “Project grants are 80% of Client E's income (2024 accounts) and each is single-year.”
- Slide 9 states operational uncertainty rather than treating launch as risk-free: “We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff.”
- Slide 10 assigns ownership and timing: “The Client E coordinator owns the launch.”

WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

COUNT: 0 high impact, 3 moderate, 0 minor

1. The board gets a launch answer before it gets the reason to choose it. [Moderate]  
   Slide 1 - “Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn.”  
   Slide 2 - “Our answer is yes, with a deliberately small start.”  
   The problem: No early sentence combines the recommended decision with the evidence-based reason it is preferable for Client E.  
   Why it matters: The board must decide whether to approve a membership launch or direct effort elsewhere. It sees the action first, while the grant exposure and proposed income contribution arrive separately, so it cannot assess why this decision follows from the problem.  
   Do this: Put one governing conclusion at the start that links the conditional launch decision to the income risk and the testable financial contribution. Use the rest of the deck to prove that conclusion.  
   Answer first: What specific income exposure does a validated membership launch address, and to what extent?

2. Nobody can tell whether £5,000 is enough or whether 8% is a meaningful conversion case. [Moderate]  
   Slide 8 - “If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one.”  
   The problem: The deck compares the 8% membership assumption with attendance, but gives no comparable conversion evidence and no benchmark showing what £5,000 means against Client E's income need.  
   Why it matters: The board could approve a launch believing it diversifies income materially, while £5,000 may be immaterial against the grant exposure. It also cannot distinguish a plausible conversion forecast from an assumption that merely looks conservative.  
   Do this: Test 8% against a relevant comparator such as Client E response history or comparable membership conversion. Compare projected income against the quantified grant shortfall or income target.  
   Aim for a line like: If the projected grant shortfall were £18,000, £5,000 would replace 28%, so the board would need to pair membership with another income action. That is the shape, not your numbers.  
   Answer first: How much income must membership replace for the board to treat it as a viable diversification measure?

3. The pilot rule does not say what to do when results miss 8% but clear 5%. [Moderate]  
   Slide 10 - “Month two: review conversion against the 8% target.”  
   Slide 10 - “Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%.”  
   The problem: The deck calls 8% the target to review but authorises full launch at 5%, without explaining the decision for results between those thresholds.  
   Why it matters: The coordinator needs a clear action after the pilot. A 6% result can be read as missing the business-case target or passing the full-launch gate, which can lead to a premature rollout instead of iteration or stopping.  
   Do this: Define one evidence-based decision rule for below-threshold, intermediate and target-level pilot results. Tie each branch to the financial case and the coordinator's capacity.  
   Answer first: What would Client E do at 4%, 6% and 8% conversion, and why?

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM

- Why is event attendance a valid comparator for paid membership conversion?
- What evidence supports the 8% assumption beyond it being lower than attendance?
- What action follows from pilot conversion below 5%, between 5% and 8%, and at or above 8%?
- How will the coordinator measure workload and member-event strain during the pilot?
