WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

COUNT: 0 high impact, 3 moderate, 0 minor

1. Nobody knows whether 5% or 8% permits a full launch [Moderate]  
   Slide 8 - "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."  
   Slide 10 - "Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."  
   The problem: The deck calls 8% the main target to test but makes 5% the full-launch condition, so it gives no decision rule for a pilot result between those figures.  
   Why it matters: At 6%, the board could approve a full launch because it clears 5%, or hold because it misses 8%. Those choices create different staffing commitments and member promises for Client E.  
   Do this: Choose one conversion gate for the full-launch decision. State whether 8% is a forecast, an aspiration or a pass condition, then pre-commit the action for results below, between and above the threshold.  
   Answer first: At what conversion rate does Client E commit to launch, defer or adjust the offer?

2. Nobody can tell whether £5,000 makes up enough of the grant gap [Moderate]  
   Slide 3 - "Two of five grants are not expected to renew, per the funder letters on file."  
   Slide 8 - "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one."  
   The problem: The deck never compares the projected £5,000 with the value of grants at risk, and its 8% conversion assumption has no membership-conversion comparator.  
   Why it matters: The board could treat membership as an answer to the income risk when it covers only a small share of the gap, or dismiss a useful pilot because the assumption cannot be interpreted. The financial scale changes what income action Client E should take.  
   Do this: Set projected membership income beside the annual income at risk from nonrenewing grants. Support a conversion range with a relevant comparator and state what a low result would indicate.  
   Aim for a line like: "If two grants leave a £20,000 annual gap, £5,000 covers 25%, so membership is one part of the response; if they leave £4,000, it can close the gap." that is the shape, not your numbers.  
   Answer first: What annual income shortfall will the two nonrenewing grants create?

3. The staff-risk test does not measure staff capacity [Moderate]  
   Slide 7 - "These use capacity Client E already has."  
   Slide 9 - "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."  
   The problem: The deck asserts that benefits fit existing capacity and names staff strain as a risk, but defines no workload measure or capacity limit for the soft launch.  
   Why it matters: The board may approve benefits that the coordinator cannot sustain, then have to reduce service after members join. A measured capacity limit could change the tier design, launch pace or decision to scale.  
   Do this: During the soft launch, measure coordinator hours for enrolment, support and event delivery against available hours. Define the workload level that pauses the launch or removes a benefit.  
   Aim for a line like: "If the soft launch needs 6 coordinator hours weekly against 4 available, pause full launch or remove the highest-load benefit." that is the shape, not your numbers.  
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours per week can the launch use without disrupting existing work?
