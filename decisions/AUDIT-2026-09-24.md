## 3. End-to-end, what a consultant actually does

This is the whole product. There is no app, no login and no page.

**A student, Sunday night, deck due Monday:**

1. Opens **a Google Drive folder** and finds three files: `COVER-SHEET.md`,
   `REVIEWER-SETUP.txt`, `TEAM-INPUT-TEMPLATE.txt`.
2. Reads the cover sheet. One page: what to do, the line to check, where to get a fresh copy, and
   what the tool refuses to do (no grade, no score, no readiness level — with the reason).
3. Opens a **new chat** on whatever model they have and pastes **`REVIEWER-SETUP.txt` as one
   message**. That is the system prompt and the context pack together, about 9,600 tokens.
4. Fills in `TEAM-INPUT-TEMPLATE.txt` — client type, artifact type, the question, the audience,
   constraints, what they are unsure about, sources, then their deck as text — and pastes it.
5. **Types their own read**: what they think the weakest part is, two or three sentences.
   **Without this the reviewer refuses**, and it cannot be argued past.
6. **Checks the first line of the reply reads exactly `KEYS: <part1>/<part2>`**, comparing against
   the cover sheet. If it does not, part of the paste was silently dropped — start a new chat and
   paste again.
7. Reads the review: an honest reaction to their own read, what works, **2–5 findings** each with a
   verbatim quote and a location, a count line, what the review could not judge, questions worth
   asking the team, and how to check the feedback.
8. Pastes the record block back, marking each finding keep / ask / cut.

**A team lead** does the same from GitHub rather than Drive, with `MODE: lead`: no typed attempt
required, denser findings, no coaching section.

**What that produces today, on the current prompt** — an actual finding, from this morning's run:

> **1. Nobody can tell whether the conversion targets are credible.** [Moderate]
> Slide 8 — *"The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."*
> **The problem:** The deck treats event attendance as support for an 8% paid-membership conversion
> assumption, but does not establish that the two behaviours are comparable.
> **Why it matters:** The board must choose whether to launch and what result should trigger
> expansion. An attendance rate measures participation in events, while the proposed thresholds
> decide whether Client E invests existing coordinator capacity in a wider paid offer.
> **Do this:** Set out the evidence connecting the target audience to the conversion threshold,
> then distinguish the pilot success measure from the assumption it is testing.
> **Answer first:** What evidence supports 8% and 5% as appropriate thresholds for paid
> membership, rather than for event attendance?

## 4. What actually blocks pilot one

### Blocker 1 — the Drive folder does not exist. This is the only true hard stop.

`build-bundle.js --release` **refuses right now**:

> `REFUSED: DRIVE_FOLDER_URL is not set in build-bundle.js.`

There are **zero releases**. `releases/` does not exist. Step 1 of the student journey above has no
destination, and the link is **baked into every bundle issued** — including into the refusal a
student sees when their paste was cut — so it cannot be added afterwards to copies already handed
out. That is why the gate is structural rather than a reminder.

**It is a human task on the owner's Google account**, roughly fifteen minutes: create one fixed
folder, "anyone with the link — viewer", set the constant, rebuild.
[30-publishing.md](../../tools/quality-reviewer/v2/30-publishing.md) section 3 has the steps, and
section 4 has a copy-out diff check that must not be skipped.

### Blocker 2 — the verification is stale, and half of it never ran

Ticket 20 verified the shipped configuration **6 of 6 on Haiku 4.5** and **0 arms on Codex** — the
account hit its usage limit. That half has still not run: `ticket-20/runs/` contains six Haiku
files and a `codex-blocked/` directory.

**And the Haiku half is now stale too.** Ticket 26 changed `CHAT-CONTEXT.md` today — the finding
template, the deleted habit section, the hardened `Do this:`, **+383 tokens**. The six arms that
proved the attempt gate refuses, lead mode passes and the interlock fires were run against a prompt
that no longer exists. The gate and interlock text were not edited, but paste 2's length and content
were, and the interlock is length-sensitive by nature.

**Cost to clear: one script, both families, about twenty minutes of runtime.** `ticket-20/run-codex.sh`
needs its binary path updated first — Codex self-updated and deleted `247581e40ee272fb`; the working
binary is `80f78947ad880e6e` (codex-cli 0.155), already fixed in ticket 26's copy.

### Blocker 3 — new today, and it is on the one refusal path a student meets

[Ticket 29](issues/29-the-interlock-false-alarmed-on-a-complete-bundle.md): the truncation interlock
**refused a complete, byte-identical bundle**, then reviewed the same bytes on re-run. Ticket 12
measured false alarms at **0 of 14**; this is the first, and it is **stochastic on identical
input** — the same property that retired the readiness level.

A student who hits it is told their paste was cut and sent back to the Drive folder when nothing was
wrong. One occurrence, one family, on the new Codex binary. **It needs a rate, not a verdict**, and
the honest options are to bound it before the pilot or to ship knowing it and watch for it.

## 5. What is *not* blocking, despite looking like it

- **The HTML output / ticket 27.** A release contains exactly three files plus a manifest:
  `REVIEWER-SETUP.txt`, `TEAM-INPUT-TEMPLATE.txt`, `COVER-SHEET.md`. **`PASTE-2C` is not in a
  release at all.** Ticket 13 settled the artifact path as an *optional extra* reached by pasting
  one more thing after the review has arrived, with prose as the default. It can ship after the
  pilot starts without touching anything a student already has.
- **[Ticket 28](issues/28-stop-do-this-handing-over-pasteable-text.md)**, the `Do this:` leak. Real
  and on one family, but mild after today's hardening: short parentheticals rather than full slide
  sentences. A judgement call, not a stop.
- **The eight parked tickets and the six accepted gaps.** Carried deliberately, each recorded.

## 6. Can we finish? Yes — but "finish" is two different finishes

**The artifact is about a day of work away.** In order:

1. Create the Drive folder and set the constant *(owner, ~15 min, nobody else can do it)*.
2. Re-run ticket 20's six arms on the current prompt, both families *(~20 min)*.
3. Decide ticket 29 — bound the false-alarm rate, or ship and watch *(one call, plus runs if bounding)*.
4. `--release`, publish to Drive and GitHub, run the section-4 copy-out diff.

**The pilot itself is not a day away, and not because of the software.** *Which chapter, how many
teams, over what period, and what trips a stop* is **still fog on this map** — it is written down as
fog in "Not yet specified", not as a ticket, and it has been fog since charting. The instrumentation
is ready (ticket 10 fixed three instruments and pre-registered their response-rate floors), but
nobody has named a cohort.

So the honest answer to "how close are we": **the thing is buildable and handable-out this week. The
pilot starts when someone names the chapter and the teams**, and that decision has no ticket and no
owner-date on it.

## 7. What the pilot will not be able to tell you

Carried from ticket 25's accepted-gaps list, because it shapes what "success" can mean:

- **The path that ships has never been scored, on any version.** All thirty-six reviews on record
  ran the harness path; the pilot runs the chat path, and the derived scorer cannot read it.
- **Must-catch recall and gold coverage are unmeasured**, not zero, and must not be quoted.
- **No release gate**, so a future member can edit the prompt with nothing telling them they broke
  it — and total turnover inside three years is the standing condition.
- **No comparison group** for the attempt-overlap measure, so convergence of student attempts toward
  the reviewer evidences little with one arm.

## 8. One thing this audit found that nobody had written down

**The tool now has a measured split by model family, and the pilot cannot choose its students'
model.** Ticket 26 established it on the finding register — a device that worked 6 of 10 on one
family and 2 of 10 on the other, with three wordings failing to move it — and today's interlock
false alarm also landed on one family only.

Every number this project holds about output quality was measured on a mixture of families with the
harness confounded (subagent framing on the Claude arms, piped process on Codex). That is recorded
on individual tickets. **It has never been stated as a property of the product**, and for a pilot
where students bring their own free accounts, it is one.
