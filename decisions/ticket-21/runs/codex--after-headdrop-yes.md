WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The deck presents the 8% conversion assumption as conservative without evidence that attendance predicts willingness to pay. [High impact]  
   Where: Slide 8  
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."  
   Why it costs the client: The board may treat 8% as evidence-based when the comparison only shows engagement, not purchase intent. That weakens the central revenue forecast and could make the proposed return appear safer than it is.  
   What to do: Label 8% explicitly as a planning hypothesis. Show outcomes across a plausible range and explain what evidence, if any, connects attendance with paid membership.  
   Answer first: What observed behaviour supports any particular conversion rate from this mailing list?

2. The financial case stops at gross revenue and does not show net contribution or the effect on grant dependence. [High impact]  
   Where: Slide 8  
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one."  
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot tell whether the scheme generates meaningful unrestricted income after events, discounts, payment fees and staff time, or whether 5,000 pounds materially changes the organisation's funding exposure.  
   What to do: Add a small scenario model showing conversion, gross revenue, incremental cost, staff time, net contribution and the resulting income mix. Keep every assumption visible.  
   Answer first: What minimum net contribution would make launching and operating the scheme worthwhile?

3. The staff-capacity risk is assumed away rather than assessed. [High impact]  
   Where: Slide 9  
   Quote: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."  
   Why it costs the client: Existing capacity is the binding constraint, so an unsupported capacity assumption leaves the board without assurance that one part-time coordinator can administer recruitment, benefits, events, renewals and member queries.  
   What to do: Estimate recurring and launch hours by activity, identify peak periods, and attach a mitigation or stop condition to any workload the coordinator cannot absorb. Treat list accuracy separately as a measurable data-quality risk.  
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours can the scheme use each month without displacing core work?

4. The warm-contact pilot cannot, by itself, validate conversion across the full mailing list or workload at full scale. [Moderate]  
   Where: Slide 9  
   Quote: "A soft launch to the most engaged 200 contacts tests both before the full launch."  
   Why it costs the client: The most engaged contacts are likely to respond differently from the remaining 1,200, while servicing a small pilot may not reveal the operational burden of a full launch. The test could therefore give false confidence on both demand and capacity.  
   What to do: State precisely what the pilot can establish, avoid extrapolating its conversion rate without adjustment, and define a separate operational test for the workload expected at scale.  
   Answer first: What evidence would justify applying results from the most engaged 200 contacts to the rest of the list?

5. The launch decision rule uses two unexplained thresholds and gives too much weight to a very small result. [High impact]  
   Where: Slide 10  
   Quote: "Month one: soft launch to 200 contacts. Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."  
   Why it costs the client: It is unclear why missing the 8% target but reaching 5% warrants expansion. At 5%, the decision would rest on only 10 members and 450 pounds of gross revenue, so chance and selection effects could materially influence the result.  
   What to do: Reconcile the target and threshold, tie the gate to net contribution and workload as well as conversion, and specify what happens in the ambiguous middle case.  
   Answer first: Why is 5% sufficient evidence to expand when the business case is built around 8%?

6. The comparator evidence supports a price range but not yet the choice of 45 pounds or the broader viability claim. [Moderate]  
   Where: Slide 5  
   Quote: "Three comparable local arts non-profits run membership at 40 to 60 pounds a year (their public websites, 2025)."  
   Why it costs the client: Price alone does not show whether the organisations are comparable in audience, benefits, scale, renewal performance or administrative burden. The board cannot see whether 45 pounds represents matched value or merely sits inside a range.  
   What to do: Show why each organisation is comparable and compare tier price, benefits and relevant operating characteristics. Do not use public pricing as evidence of conversion unless take-up data exists.  
   Answer first: Which observable similarities make these organisations useful comparators for Client E?

THE HABIT TO BUILD

Separate facts, inferences and hypotheses before turning analysis into a recommendation. The weak habit here is allowing a plausible proxy—attendance—to inherit the status of evidence for a different behaviour—payment. As an exercise, build a four-column table for each important number: claim, direct evidence, assumption and decision trigger.

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE

- Whether the accounts, box-office data, funder letters and comparator websites were transcribed accurately.
- Whether 45 pounds is acceptable to Client E's audience.
- Whether the proposed benefits are attractive enough to sustain renewals.
- Whether local partners have agreed to provide discounts or what administering them would require.
- The mailing list's accuracy, consent status, engagement distribution or duplication rate.
- Whether unprovided appendices or visuals contain further financial or capacity analysis.
- Which membership model is best for this arts context beyond what the deck itself establishes.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM

1. What evidence connects event attendance with willingness to buy membership?
2. What is the net income at 3%, 5% and 8% conversion after all incremental costs?
3. How many staff hours are required during launch and in a normal operating month?
4. Why does the full-launch threshold differ from the headline target?
5. What did the comparators reveal beyond their advertised prices?
