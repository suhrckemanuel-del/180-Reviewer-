WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The 8% conversion assumption is not yet supported by evidence that can carry the recommendation. [High impact]  
   Where: Slide 8  
   Quote: “The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test.”  
   Why it costs the client: Attendance and paid-membership conversion are different behaviours with different denominators. Calling 8% conservative may give the board more confidence than the evidence warrants and makes the projected income appear firmer than it is.  
   What to do: Present 8% explicitly as an unvalidated planning case. Show a small conversion range, explain what each case produces and make the pilot responsible for selecting the usable assumption.  
   Answer first: What evidence connects event attendance to willingness to pay £45 for these particular benefits?

2. The financial case shows gross revenue but not whether membership is materially worthwhile after costs and staff time. [High impact]  
   Where: Slide 8  
   Quote: “If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one.”  
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot compare membership with the income risk it is meant to address or judge whether £5,000 is worth the payment fees, event costs, partner management and coordinator time required to earn it.  
   What to do: Add a compact scenario model showing gross income, direct costs, staff hours, net contribution and the proportion of the threatened income gap covered under low, central and high conversion cases.  
   Answer first: What is the minimum net contribution that would make this scheme worthwhile for Client E?

3. The pilot uses two different success thresholds without explaining the decision logic. [High impact]  
   Where: Slide 10  
   Quote: “Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%.”  
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot tell whether 8% is the forecast, the target or the launch requirement. A result between 5% and 8% could therefore be described as both underperformance and permission to proceed.  
   What to do: Define one decision rule with clearly distinguished forecast, minimum viable threshold and stretch result. Derive the launch threshold from the economics and capacity requirements rather than choosing it in isolation.  
   Answer first: At what conversion rate does the pilot cover its costs and justify the coordinator’s ongoing workload?

4. The capacity risk is acknowledged but not actually tested or quantified. [High impact]  
   Where: Slide 9  
   Quote: “We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff.”  
   Why it costs the client: Existing capacity is the binding constraint, yet the deck provides no estimate of setup or recurring hours. A 200-contact email test also cannot by itself demonstrate the workload of servicing members and delivering two events.  
   What to do: Map the launch and recurring tasks, estimate coordinator hours by month and identify which benefit creates each workload. Use the pilot to test administration, while separately testing event capacity with an operational plan or small trial.  
   Answer first: Which current activities would be reduced or stopped if membership requires more coordinator time than expected?

5. The evidence for the £45 price cannot be traced from the deck. [Moderate]  
   Where: Slide 5  
   Quote: “Three comparable local arts non-profits run membership at 40 to 60 pounds a year (their public websites, 2025).”  
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot check whether the organisations are genuinely comparable, what their fees include or whether £45 reflects the value of Client E’s proposed offer rather than merely sitting inside a broad range.  
   What to do: Name the comparators in a source note, record the access date and compare their relevant features, scale and benefits. Explain how that evidence informed £45 while retaining the pilot as the test of willingness to pay.  
   Answer first: On which characteristics are these three organisations comparable to Client E?

THE HABIT TO BUILD

Separate an assumption from the evidence used to test it. The weak habit here is turning a plausible number into a recommendation before defining the decision rule around it. Practise building a one-page assumption table for each recommendation: evidence, uncertainty, low/base/high cases, test method and the result that would change the decision.
