WHAT WORKS
1. The decision is clear and actionable. "Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn." (Slide 1). The board receives a specific recommendation with a timeframe and a scope.

2. The implementation plan includes built-in validation gates. "Month one: soft launch to 200 contacts. Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%." (Slide 10). This is disciplined risk management: full rollout is not committed until soft-launch data arrives.

3. The team is explicit about the biggest assumption. "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test." (Slide 9). Naming the assumption that matters most is rare and valuable.

WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The 8% conversion target carries no comparator, leaving the client unable to judge whether it is realistic. [High impact]
   Where: Slides 8, 9
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one. The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The revenue projection and the soft-launch gate (5% threshold) both rest on this assumption. The board cannot evaluate whether 8% is too ambitious, too modest, or grounded in sector reality without knowing what comparable organisations actually convert at. "Conservative against 32% attendance" is logic, not a comparator. The client will not know whether hitting 5% on the soft launch means the model is sound or struggling.
   What to do: Name a comparator for 8%. What do membership programmes at similar arts non-profits actually achieve? If that data is not available, state what would count as evidence that 8% is the right target. Do not anchor to "it feels conservative." Say: "32% of our list attended events, 8% will convert to paid because [reason], and we would expect [benchmark] if that holds true."
   Answer first: What conversion rates do comparable arts non-profits see on membership soft launches, and what baseline is Client E willing to accept as a signal of health?

THE HABIT TO BUILD
The team can scope down risk (single tier, not three) and phases implementation with built-in gates. The next level: find and ground assumptions before you design the implementation, not during it. When a decision rests on a number you cannot source, stop and ask: what data would make us confident this is right? Invest the hours before you assume it. This team put assumptions into the soft-launch design. Show the same discipline finding what the assumptions should rest on.

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE
- Whether 8% is a realistic conversion rate for this sector on a soft launch, or what other arts organisations actually achieve
- Whether Client E's mailing list is fresh, accurate and engaged enough to convert at this rate. The team plans to test it; a lead with direct knowledge of Client E would have better sight.
- Whether the part-time coordinator can absorb two members-only events a year at 100+ member scale without help. The team assumes capacity exists; capacity math is not shown.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM
1. What conversion rate would you expect to see on the soft launch if your 8% full-launch assumption is sound? Why 5% specifically as the gate?
2. Have you asked other local arts non-profits what their membership conversion actually looked like in the first month?
3. What does "members-only events do not strain staff" look like in hours per week? At what member count would you need to add capacity?
