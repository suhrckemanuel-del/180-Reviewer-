WHAT WORKS

- The deck answers the client's central question directly. Slide 2: "Our answer is yes, with a deliberately small start."
- The team states its key operational assumption rather than presenting it as fact. Slide 9: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
- Initial ownership is explicit. Slide 10: "The Client E coordinator owns the launch."

WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The scale-up decision contradicts itself: a 6% result would pass the 5% launch rule but fail the 8% review target. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target."
   Quote: "Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot apply both rules. Under one reading a 6% result means pause or revise. Under the other it means proceed to full launch.
   What to do: Set one decision threshold or define distinct roles for the two percentages. Tie each threshold to a named action and an evidence-based rationale.
   Answer first: Exactly what should Client E do if conversion is between 5% and 8%?

2. The financial case does not show whether £5,000 would materially diversify income, and its conversion comparator is not like-for-like. [High impact]
   Where: Slides 3 and 8
   Quote: "Project grants are 80% of Client E's income (2024 accounts) and each is single-year."
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one."
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot judge whether membership is worth the workload without comparing net proceeds to the grant income at risk, total income or a minimum useful contribution. Event attendance does not establish willingness to pay.
   What to do: Compare expected net membership income with the funding gap and delivery cost. Support the conversion range with relevant membership evidence or label it as a scenario rather than a conservative estimate.
   Answer first: What minimum net unrestricted income would make this scheme worthwhile for Client E?

3. The proposed benefits are not connected to evidence that the target contacts would pay £45 for them. [High impact]
   Where: Slides 4 and 7
   Quote: "This is an engaged base to convert."
   Quote: "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners."
   Why it costs the client: Engagement with events does not show demand for this package. The answer could change the benefits, the price or whether the scheme should launch at all.
   What to do: Add proportionate evidence of willingness to pay and benefit preference. Use the soft launch to test alternative offer elements, not only one fixed package.
   Answer first: What evidence shows that the most engaged contacts value these benefits enough to pay £45?

4. Existing staff capacity is asserted, while the pilot cannot test the workload of operating the full scheme. [High impact]
   Where: Slides 7 and 9
   Quote: "These use capacity Client E already has."
   Quote: "A soft launch to the most engaged 200 contacts tests both before the full launch."
   Why it costs the client: A small launch can reveal setup effort but not necessarily the recurring administration and event load at scale. The board may approve benefits that the part-time coordinator cannot sustain.
   What to do: Estimate setup and recurring hours by activity. Define a capacity limit and contingency, then test the highest-risk operational task during the pilot.
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours would setup, renewals, enquiries, partner management and two events require?

5. The complete action-and-reason insight arrives on Slide 6 rather than in the opening answer. [Minor]
   Where: Slides 1 and 6
   Quote: "Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn."
   Quote: "Launching one tier at 45 pounds, rather than three tiers at once, keeps admin low and lets Client E learn before it scales."
   Why it costs the client: On the mechanical skim of titles and first sentences, the board sees the answer immediately but must wait until Slide 6 for the reason behind the chosen design.
   What to do: Bring the governing action and its reason together at the start. Keep Slide 6 for supporting evidence and implications.
   Answer first: What single rationale should the board remember for choosing this launch design?

THE HABIT TO BUILD

Stop treating an available percentage as evidence for a different behaviour. The weak habit is moving from attendance to paid conversion without testing whether the measures are comparable. For every decision-driving figure, write three answers: compared with what, what the comparison means and which client action changes as a result.

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE

The visual hierarchy, layout and readability because only extracted text was provided.
Whether the accounts, box-office data, funder letters and comparable pricing sources support the cited statements.
Actual willingness to pay because no member research or pilot evidence was included.
Net financial contribution because delivery costs were not provided.
Operational feasibility because no workload or capacity estimate was included.
Whether 200 contacts provide a reliable pilot result because the selection method and statistical rationale were not provided.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM

1. Which conversion threshold governs scale-up, and what evidence supports it?
2. How much grant income is at risk in pounds?
3. What net contribution would justify the coordinator's time?
4. What evidence supports the £45 price and proposed benefits?
5. How will the 200 pilot contacts be selected, and what outcome would trigger revision rather than launch or cancellation?
