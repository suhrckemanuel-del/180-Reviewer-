WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. Ask the board to authorize a pilot, or justify why the evidence already supports committing to a full scheme. [High impact]
   Where: Slides 1, 8 and 10
   Quote: Slide 1: "Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn."
   Quote: Slide 8: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Quote: Slide 10: "Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The board may approve an ongoing scheme when the analysis currently supports a reversible demand test. On the required title-and-first-sentence skim, the launch is clear but its conditional status and reason are not.
   What to do: Separate the decision to test from the later decision to scale. Put the immediate action, its reason and the evidence gate in one governing sentence near the start.
   Answer first: Is the board being asked to approve a three-month pilot or an ongoing membership scheme?

2. Ground the 8% forecast and 5% launch gate in relevant evidence rather than the attendance rate. [High impact]
   Where: Slides 8 and 10
   Quote: Slide 8: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one."
   Quote: Slide 10: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target."
   Quote: Slide 10: "Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: An arbitrary threshold could make the client scale an uneconomic scheme or abandon a viable one. Event attendance is a different behaviour from purchasing an annual membership and does not establish that 8% is conservative.
   What to do: Treat 8% as a hypothesis unless supported by comparable membership conversion or Client E's previous paid-response data. Reconcile 5% with 8% and define success through net income, workload and retention as well as sign-ups.
   Answer first: What observed conversion evidence or minimum viable net return makes 5% and 8% suitable decision thresholds?

3. Validate the £45 offer itself, not only its position within competitors' price range. [Moderate]
   Where: Slides 5 and 7
   Quote: Slide 5: "Three comparable local arts non-profits run membership at 40 to 60 pounds a year (their public websites, 2025)."
   Quote: Slide 7: "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners."
   Why it costs the client: Low conversion could reflect the price or benefits rather than rejection of membership. Without distinguishing those explanations, the pilot may produce a result the board cannot interpret.
   What to do: Separate competitor pricing from evidence of willingness to pay. Show how the comparators' offers resemble Client E's and define how the soft launch will test the proposed price and benefits.
   Answer first: What evidence shows that the target contacts value this benefit bundle at £45?

4. Replace the capacity assumption with a workload test before fixing the benefit package. [Moderate]
   Where: Slides 7 and 9
   Quote: Slide 7: "These use capacity Client E already has."
   Quote: Slide 9: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
   Why it costs the client: With one part-time coordinator and no hiring budget, underestimated administration could force the client to reduce benefits, delay launch or divert effort from existing programmes.
   What to do: Map the recurring tasks, ownership and monthly hours at pilot and full-launch volumes. Use that estimate to simplify, phase or remove benefits before the board approves the operating model.
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours per month can membership consume without displacing current work?

THE HABIT TO BUILD

Separate the decision to test from the decision to scale. The weak habit is turning an acknowledged hypothesis into a launch recommendation. For each recommendation, write three lines as an exercise: the decision required now, the evidence supporting it and the measurable gate for the next decision.
