WHAT WORKS

The recommendation is clear and proportionate. Slide 1 is direct: "Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn." This lands with the board immediately and the team has not muddied it.

The staged approach shows honest risk awareness. The soft launch to 200 contacts followed by a review gate is a real de-risking move, not a rhetorical one.

The team states what they are unsure about. Slide 8 says "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test." This is the habit of experienced reviewers, and it is missing often enough to praise when it appears.

WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The 8% conversion assumption lacks derivation. The team says it is "conservative against the 32% attendance rate" but does not show why. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8.
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one. The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The board is being asked to sign off on a business case that hinges on one number. The team's reasoning for that number is stated as a comparison (8% vs 32%) but not as a derivation (why we expect 25% of attendees to convert to payers). Without that, the board cannot assess whether the assumption is conservative or a guess that happened to feel low.
   What to do: Show the reasoning. Does the team have drop-off in similar transitions elsewhere? Have they looked at what happens between "attends an event" and "pays to attend events they could attend free"? What comparables tell them? Lay out the logic. If it is partly intuition, say so. The board can live with calibrated judgment. They cannot live with assumptions that are stated but not shown.
   Answer first: What conversion pathways exist in the data (grant attendees → members, regular participants → members, one-time visitors → members) and what rate does each suggest?

2. The soft launch threshold (5%) is lower than the full-launch assumption (8%), and the team does not explain the decision rule. [Moderate impact]
   Where: Slide 10 and Slide 8.
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: This is a disconnect that looks like an accidental calibration. If 8% is conservative and necessary, why proceed to full launch on 5%? If 5% is acceptable, why design the business case around 8%? The board needs to know whether this is a deliberate two-stage sensitivity test (soft launch clears the lower bar, full launch aims for the original target) or an error. A launch decision made on the wrong threshold can waste time and damage trust with members.
   What to do: Name the decision rule. If the logic is "soft launch clears 5%, we have proof of concept and will optimize toward 8% in full launch," state it. If it is "8% is the full-launch minimum and 5% is a 'go/no-go' point lower than that," say why that's defensible given the staff constraint. Make the threshold explicit and say what it means for the go/no-go call.
   Answer first: What does a 5% soft-launch result tell you that a 6% or 4% result would not? Why is that the pivot point?

3. The assumption that members-only events do not strain staff is stated but not examined. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 9 and Slide 7.
   Quote: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff." And: "Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners. These use capacity Client E already has."
   Why it costs the client: The known constraint is "one part-time coordinator." Two members-only events a year, managing priority booking, handling a member database and comms, and running the soft launch and review cycle is not a passive use of existing capacity. If the soft launch reaches 112 members (5% of 1,400 × 8% is 112), the team now has a member base to service. The coordinator's actual workload in months 1–3 is not estimated anywhere. A launch that exhausts the one coordinator and forces Client E to hire staff before the revenue justifies it is not a small start.
   What to do: Map the coordinator's current workload, estimate the hours for soft-launch execution (messaging, sign-ups, event logistics, member comms, data handling), and show the margin. If there is none, be honest: this launch requires temporary help or a shift in other duties. That is not a blocker, but the board needs to know it.
   Answer first: How many hours a month does the soft launch and member management require, and how much existing space does the coordinator have?

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE

Whether a price point of 45 pounds is right for this community. The comparables are three local groups, all doing this already, but without knowing their size, programming style, or member churn, it is hard to say whether Client E should match them or charge differently.

Whether the risk of list inaccuracy (named in Slide 9) is real. If the 1,400-person list is old or duplicate-heavy, the accessible base is smaller and 8% becomes different. The team may know this well. The board should hear the estimate of list quality.

Whether a two-year trend in grant renewals is enough to justify diversification urgency. The team cites two of five grants at risk, but does not say whether that is a new pattern or a standing 40% churn.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM

1. What proportion of the 1,400 mailing-list contacts have attended an event in the last 12 months, and what is the overlap between "attendees" and "likely members"?

2. If the soft launch hits exactly 5%, do you launch to the full 1,200? What is the minimum result that would stop the rollout?

3. What is the staffing plan if the soft launch succeeds? Month 3 is both a launch month and your first review month; is that timeline realistic for one coordinator?

4. Do you have churn assumptions for the members-only events? (Do people who feel excluded or underwhelmed drop off? How long do members stay?)
