WHAT WORKS

The framing is sharp. Slide 3 names the exact problem: "Two of five grants are not expected to renew." This makes the revenue case concrete and urgent. The single-tier approach (Slide 6) is an honest risk reduction that a board will recognize.

The implementation structure is real: three months, a gate at month two, a soft launch before full rollout. The soft launch itself is a strong instinct and it tests the two core assumptions the team knows matter.

WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The first month of the launch is titled but not spelled out [High impact]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "Month one: soft launch to 200 contacts."
   Why it costs the client: The coordinator cannot act on this without coming back for the message, the channel (email, post, call?), the timing, and what success in the soft launch looks like. The recommendation stops at the title of the first step.
   What to do: Draft the actual outreach—one version the coordinator can send, or one template they can tailor. Name the channel, the timing (before or after a specific date), and what opens month two: do you count replies, purchases, or both? Name what "soft" means to this launch.
   Answer first: What does the coordinator actually send to the 200, and when?

2. The 8% conversion assumption rests on a flawed comparison [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: Event attendance and membership purchase are different funnels. That 32% opened an email or saw a listing and chose to attend a free event. Membership requires a different decision. Saying 8% is conservative against 32% does not ground the 8% or justify it to the board. The team flagged this as their own uncertainty, and the deck does not resolve it.
   What to do: Either source the 8% (find one comparable non-profit's conversion from mailing list to member), or reframe it as a test threshold rather than a claim. Replace the comparison with a clearer path: "We know 32% will come to events. Of those, some will value membership. We assume 1 in 4 engaged attendees becomes a member, which gives us 8%. Month two will tell us if that holds." Then let the data speak.
   Answer first: Where does the 8% number come from, or why should the board treat it as a justified target rather than a hypothesis?

3. The soft-launch decision rule is undefined [High impact]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "if the soft launch clears 5%"
   Why it costs the client: Five percent of what? If it is 5% of the 200 (ten members), does ten members justify the cost of two events? The deck doesn't connect the soft-launch outcome to the decision. Month two becomes unclear: what comparison decides month three?
   What to do: Define the gate explicitly. Name whether 5% means absolute (ten members from 200) or relative to a segment. Add the arithmetic: if we convert ten members at 45 pounds and run two events that cost X hours and Y pounds, is this worth proceeding? The coordinator needs that math to hold a conversation with the board in month two.
   Answer first: What does the data from the soft launch have to show to justify moving to month three, and why?

4. The capacity assumption for members-only events carries unquantified cost [Major]
   Where: Slide 7 and Slide 9
   Quote: "These use capacity Client E already has" and "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
   Why it costs the client: Two events a year for a part-time coordinator: is that 5 hours each, 20 hours each? If it is 20, the coordinator is running 40 hours a year on membership alone. If the coordinator is 0.5 FTE at 1,000 hours a year, that is 4% of their time. If the events are more work, the model breaks and the recommendation needs to change.
   What to do: Estimate the delivery cost of each members-only event in hours. Compare that to the coordinator's available capacity (given the brief). If the events are feasible, the deck can say so and be done. If they are borderline, the recommendation might need to start with a simpler benefit structure (priority booking only, no events in year one).
   Answer first: How many hours does each members-only event cost Client E, and what is the coordinator's available time after grant work?

THE HABIT TO BUILD

The team has the shape of an implementation plan but not the grain. Move from high-level timeframes to concrete actions: what email reads, what the coordinator actually does, what the numbers say at the gate, what happens if you miss them. The habit: for every month and every decision rule, ask "could the client do this on Monday?" and draft what Monday looks like.

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE

- Whether comparable organisations' membership models are comparable to Client E's (sector maturity, reach, pricing context)
- Whether two events a year are typical for membership schemes at this price point
- Whether the board will perceive 5,000 pounds as material to the revenue mix
- What the conversion rate actually is among engaged contacts (no local data to source it against)

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM

1. Did you run the soft launch 5% threshold by Client E? Do they think ten members is meaningful or is it too low?
2. Can you draft the outreach message the coordinator would send? Does it work as text?
3. What do the event costs actually look like, in hours and money? Do you have a budget figure from Client E?
4. Have you asked whether the mailing list is actively maintained? If 200 of the 1,400 are disengaged, that changes the soft launch math.
5. Where did you land on the 8% as you wrote this? Do you feel it is solid enough to put in front of the board?
