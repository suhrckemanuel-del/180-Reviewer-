WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The revenue case rests on an 8% conversion assumption that is not validated against paid behavior or Client E's income need. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one."
   Where: Slide 8
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Why it costs the client: The board may expect about 5,000 pounds when the deck has shown engagement, not willingness to pay. Without net revenue and a diversification benchmark, it cannot tell whether an 8% result justifies the coordinator time.
   What to do: Treat 8% as a test scenario rather than a supported forecast. Show a plausible conversion range, subtract incremental delivery and administration costs and compare net income with a stated diversification target. Use the pilot to estimate paid conversion.
   Answer first: What evidence links event attendance to paid membership conversion, and what net-income result would make the scheme worthwhile?

2. The 5% launch gate conflicts with the stated 8% target because the deck does not explain what happens between them. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: Between 5% and 8%, the coordinator is told both that the target was missed and that the full launch should proceed. The board cannot see which result triggers scale, iteration or stop.
   What to do: Define one decision rule with a reason. Specify what the client does below, within and above the chosen threshold, using pilot economics and workload rather than an unexplained percentage.
   Answer first: Why is 5% sufficient to launch when 8% is the target?

3. The capacity claim is asserted, then treated as an assumption, while the pilot has no stated workload measure. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 7
   Quote: "These use capacity Client E already has."
   Where: Slide 9
   Quote: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
   Why it costs the client: Client E could commit its only coordinator to benefits whose workload erases the financial value or cannot be sustained. Conversion alone cannot establish whether the scheme is feasible on existing capacity.
   What to do: Estimate hours and costs for setup, administration, events and partner management. Track those during the pilot and set a workload limit alongside the conversion gate.
   Answer first: How many additional coordinator hours can the scheme consume while remaining worthwhile and sustainable?

4. The governing insight is sound but delayed until slide 6. [Minor]
   Where: Slide 6
   Quote: "Launching one tier at 45 pounds, rather than three tiers at once, keeps admin low and lets Client E learn before it scales."
   Why it costs the client: A fast board reader sees the recommendation before learning why the single-tier pilot is the preferred risk response. This does not change the action, but it weakens the decision discussion.
   What to do: Put the complete choice-and-reason logic in the opening answer. Let later slides prove it rather than requiring the reader to assemble it.
   Answer first: What single reason most strongly justifies this particular membership design?

THE HABIT TO BUILD

Separate observations, assumptions and decision gates. The weak habit is treating an adjacent metric as validation for an assumption. For every recommendation-driving number, make a three-column table: evidence observed, assumption inferred and action triggered. If a row cannot be completed, the decision logic is not ready.

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE

The visual hierarchy, charts and page layouts because only extracted text was provided.
Whether the accounts, funder letters, box-office data and comparable pricing are accurate.
Whether the three comparators are genuinely comparable to Client E.
Whether the membership benefits are attractive to Client E's audience.
Whether partner discounts are available on workable terms.
The legal and tax treatment of membership income.
Whether other analysis or appendices contain conversion, cost or workload evidence.
Whether extraction omitted or reordered content because no extraction notes were supplied.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM

1. What makes 8% conservative for paid membership rather than merely lower than event attendance?
2. Is the estimated 5,000 pounds gross revenue or net contribution after delivery and administration?
3. What board objective defines meaningful income diversification?
4. Why does 5% justify scaling when the stated target is 8%?
5. What workload result would stop or redesign the launch even if conversion is strong?
