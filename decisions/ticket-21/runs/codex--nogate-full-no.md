WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. The deck gives the board two different go or no-go thresholds for the same pilot. [High impact]
   Where: Slide 10
   Quote: "Month two: review conversion against the 8% target."
   Quote: "Month three: full launch if the soft launch clears 5%."
   Why it costs the client: The board could authorize full rollout at 5% to 7.9% even though performance is assessed against 8%. That changes the go or no-go decision and makes the pilot rule unsafe to apply.
   What to do: Establish one decision rule or explicit performance bands. Derive them from the minimum net contribution and workload the client requires, then assign a distinct action to each outcome.
   Answer first: At what conversion rate does membership cover its incremental cost and materially diversify income?

2. The deck presents staff capacity as both established and untested. [Moderate]
   Where: Slides 7 and 9
   Quote: "These use capacity Client E already has."
   Quote: "We assume the mailing list is accurate and that members-only events do not strain staff."
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot tell whether the offer is feasible for one part-time coordinator. If spare capacity is absent, Client E must remove or redesign benefits before launch, not discover the constraint after members have paid.
   What to do: Quantify the hours and direct costs required for administration, booking and events. Identify which benefits fit existing capacity and test workload before making member commitments.
   Answer first: How many coordinator hours are available each month and how many would the proposed benefits consume?

3. The recommendation's governing reason arrives too late for the message to survive a fast pass. [Moderate]
   Where: Slides 1 and 6
   Quote: "Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the autumn."
   Quote: "Launching one tier at 45 pounds, rather than three tiers at once, keeps admin low and lets Client E learn before it scales."
   Why it costs the client: A fast reader may retain an unconditional autumn launch and miss that the intended choice is a bounded experiment designed to limit administration. The board could approve a broader commitment than the evidence supports.
   What to do: Put the action and its risk-limiting reason together at the opening. Make the later slide titles develop that same pilot-to-scale argument.
   Answer first: Should the board authorize a pilot now or authorize the full scheme?

4. The £5,000 income case is not compared with the financial need it is meant to address. [Moderate]
   Where: Slides 3 and 8
   Quote: "Project grants are 80% of Client E's income (2024 accounts) and each is single-year."
   Quote: "If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one."
   Why it costs the client: The board cannot tell whether £5,000 is meaningful diversification or a distraction. If it is small relative to the grants at risk or disappears after delivery costs, the correct decision may be not to launch.
   What to do: Compare gross membership income with the value of the grants at risk, total annual income and incremental delivery cost. Show how the conclusion changes across downside, base and upside scenarios.
   Answer first: What minimum net income would make membership worthwhile for Client E?

5. The 8% assumption is not defensible as a paid-conversion forecast and the pilot cannot validate it for the full list as designed. [Moderate]
   Where: Slides 8 and 9
   Quote: "The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing to test."
   Quote: "A soft launch to the most engaged 200 contacts tests both before the full launch."
   Why it costs the client: The board could scale from a selected high-engagement sample whose response will not hold for the remaining list. Attendance shows participation, not willingness to pay, so neither basis makes 8% defensible.
   What to do: Present 8% as a scenario rather than a forecast. Test across defined engagement segments or explain a sampling approach that supports extrapolation, then set the scaling rule before results arrive.
   Answer first: What observed paid behaviour or segment-level evidence supports conversion outside the 200 most engaged contacts?

THE HABIT TO BUILD

Turn every important assumption into a decision rule before recommending action. The weak habit is naming something as uncertain while still building the conclusion on it. As an exercise, create a one-page pilot logic table with each assumption, its evidence, the required comparator, the test measure and the action triggered by each result.

WHAT THIS REVIEW COULD NOT JUDGE

- The accuracy of the accounts, box-office data, funder letters or comparable pricing was not independently verified.
- The visual hierarchy and chart legibility could not be assessed because only extracted text was supplied.
- Whether image-only material or appendices were omitted could not be established because no extraction notes were supplied.
- Actual coordinator capacity, membership administration cost and event delivery cost were not provided.
- Whether the proposed benefits and £45 price are attractive to Client E's audience could not be established from the deck.
- The suitability of membership relative to other income-diversification options was not assessed because no alternatives analysis was supplied.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING THE TEAM

1. What is the annual value of the two grants at risk and what proportion would £5,000 replace?
2. Which threshold controls the full-launch decision: 5% or 8%, and what economics justify it?
3. How were the 200 most engaged contacts defined and how will their results be translated to the remaining 1,200?
4. What staff hours and direct costs would administration and two members-only events require?
5. What evidence indicates that attendance predicts willingness to pay £45?
